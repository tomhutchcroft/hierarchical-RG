    What the wiki argues, and three ways through it.

    On a hierarchical lattice the renormalisation group is not an approximation. A block has no boundary: every pair of vertices in different children of a block sits at the same distance, so the energy of a block is the sum of the energies of its children plus one interaction between their sums. Decimation is therefore an identity, the state of the recursion is the law of one number, and the critical behaviour is the behaviour of an orbit of a single map. Everything in this wiki follows from that.

    The argument has three moves. First, the block structure and the identity it makes exact. Second, the map and its fixed points, including the one case solved in closed form and the two rigorous analyses of the case that is not. Third, the models that are not scalar spins, where the same mechanism produces percolation exponents, a free direction for vector spins, and the obstruction that stops the argument on $\mathbb Z^d$.

    The parts

    1. Part 1: The block structure — Nested blocks, congruent children, and the decimation identity they make exact.
    2. Part 2: The renormalisation group — The map on block-spin laws, its fixed points, and the exponents read off them.
    3. Part 3: Percolation, vectors, and the Euclidean comparison — What changes when the order parameter is a set, a vector, or a field on Z^d.
