    The growth exponent follows from two pointwise bounds on the two-point function

    proposition · UTEB · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Assume, at $\beta=\beta_c$: (IR) there is $A<\infty$ with $\tau_\infty(0,y)\leq AL^{-(d-\alpha)\operatorname{lev}(y)}$ for every $y\neq0$; and (FSL) there are $a>0$ and $n_0$ with $\tau_n(0,y)\geq aL^{-(d-\alpha)n}$ for every $n\geq n_0$ and every $y$ in the top shell $\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}$. Then there are constants $0<c\leq K<\infty$, depending only on $L,d,\alpha,A,a,n_0$, with

    $$c\,L^{(d+\alpha)n}\ \leq\ V_n\ \leq\ K\,L^{(d+\alpha)n}\qquad\text{for every }n\geq1,$$

    so the growth exponent is $\kappa=\alpha$ and the rescaled second moment stays bounded away from zero and infinity.

    Proof

    Upper bound. By Griffiths monotonicity in the couplings, $\tau_n(0,y)\leq\tau_\infty(0,y)$ for $n$ with $y\in\Lambda_n$, so $V_n\leq L^{dn}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n}\tau_\infty(0,y)$. The shell at level $k$ has $(m-1)m^{k-1}$ vertices, so by (IR) the sum is at most $1+A(1-m^{-1})\sum_{k\leq n}L^{\alpha k}\leq K'L^{\alpha n}$, and $V_n\leq K'L^{(d+\alpha)n}$.

    Lower bound. Retaining only the top shell and using (FSL), $V_n\geq L^{dn}(m-1)m^{n-1}\,aL^{-(d-\alpha)n}=a(1-m^{-1})L^{(d+\alpha)n}$ for $n\geq n_0$; finitely many smaller $n$ are absorbed into the constant.

    For the percolation class on the same lattice both hypotheses are theorems: the pointwise two-point bounds at criticality, in both directions and in exactly this form, are Hutchcroft's, with the lower bound for connections inside the smallest common block — which is what (FSL) asks for. So for percolation the conclusion of the proposition is proved, and it reproduces the $\chi_n\asymp L^{\alpha n}$ of the percolation article from a different direction. For the Ising model the two hypotheses are the missing input, and the proposition says precisely what they buy and why neither is implied by what the earlier sections prove: the upper bound needs the infinite-volume pointwise decay, which sum-level information does not give, and the lower bound needs a finite-volume estimate in the opposite direction to Griffiths monotonicity, which points the other way.
