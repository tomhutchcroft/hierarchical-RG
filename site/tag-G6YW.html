    Exponents in terms of the two renormalisation-group numbers

    theorem · G6YW · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Suppose that $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ and that, uniformly in $n$ and $\beta$,

    $$\chi_n(\beta)\asymp\min\bigl\{L^{\kappa n},\ \xi(\beta)^{\kappa}\bigr\},\qquad \xi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\nu}.$$

    Then $\chi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\kappa\nu}$, that is $\gamma=\kappa\nu$. If in addition $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}$ then $2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=d\nu$, and if in addition $M\asymp\xi^{-(d-\kappa)/2}$ then $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$; under these two, together with the two relations just displayed,

    $$\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa},\qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d\nu,\qquad \gamma=\nu(2-\eta),\qquad \gamma=\beta_{\mathrm{o}}(\delta-1).$$
    Proof of the dictionary

    For $\gamma$: the supremum over $n$ of $\min\{L^{\kappa n},\xi^\kappa\}$ is $\xi^\kappa$ up to constants, and $\xi\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\nu}$ gives $\chi\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\kappa\nu}$.

    For $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}$: $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{d\nu}$, and comparing with $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}}$ gives the identity.

    For $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}$: $M\asymp\xi^{-(d-\kappa)/2}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{\nu(d-\kappa)/2}$, and comparing with the definition gives $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$, which is the same as $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-2+\eta)$ because $\kappa=2-\eta$.

    For $\delta$: Widom's relation $\gamma=\beta_{\mathrm{o}}(\delta-1)$ is the definition of $\delta$ once the other two exponents are known, and substituting $\gamma=\kappa\nu$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$ gives $\kappa\nu=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)(\delta-1)$, hence $\delta-1=\frac{2\kappa}{d-\kappa}$ and $\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa}$. The last two displays restate the first two relations with $\kappa=2-\eta$.

    Which of these is a theorem of the model and which is an extra hypothesis is the point of the small print. The first display of the dictionary is a theorem of percolation in the hierarchical model below the triangle threshold, where the block susceptibility is known to grow like $L^{\kappa n}$ and the truncated form is a consequence of the finite correlation length; hyperscaling, the identity $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}$, is the hypothesis that fails in the classical regime. The relations $\gamma=\kappa\nu$ and the identification $\kappa=2-\eta$ are renormalisation-group statements and hold whenever the fixed point exists with a finite variance; $\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa}$ follows only when hyperscaling does.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    The Gaussian fixed point. With $\kappa=\alpha$ and $\nu=1/\alpha$ the dictionary gives $\gamma=1$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\frac{d-\alpha}{2\alpha}$, $\delta=\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d/\alpha$. These are the classical long-range values and they satisfy all three scaling relations identically; see the Gaussian article, where they are computed from the recursion rather than from the dictionary.

    Hierarchical percolation. Here $\kappa=\alpha$ is a theorem for every $0<\alpha<d$ [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021], so $\eta=2-\alpha$ and $\gamma=\nu\alpha$. For $\alpha<d/3$ the model is mean-field in the sense that the triangle condition holds and $\delta=2$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=1$, while $\nu=1/\alpha$; hyperscaling fails there, and indeed $\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}<2$ for $\alpha<d/3$. For $\alpha>d/3$ the volume exponent satisfies $\delta\geq\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}>2$, with equality conjectured, and the value of $\nu$ is not known. Details are in the percolation article.

    The Ising model. For $\alpha<d/2$ the exponents are the classical ones, $\nu=1/\alpha$ and $\kappa=\alpha$, by Aizenman and Fernandez [@aizenman-fernandez-1988] and, in the Euclidean case, by the lace expansion [@heydenreich-hofstad-sakai-2008]. For $\alpha>d/2$ the critical behaviour is governed by the non-Gaussian fixed point, $\nu$ differs from $1/\alpha$, and the growth exponent stays at $\kappa=\alpha$ by the theorem above. At $\alpha=d/2$ the classical values hold with logarithmic corrections.

    Non-example: a model without a finite-variance fixed point. If the rescaled orbit diverges, as it does for $\beta>\beta_c$ in the Gaussian model, no exponent is defined at that intensity: the block spin per site tends to a nonzero limit and the susceptibility is infinite. The definitions above are statements about the critical orbit and its neighbourhood only.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    Finite-size scaling. The block susceptibility at criticality is the finite-size susceptibility of the block, so $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ is a finite-size scaling statement with $\kappa=\gamma/\nu$: the susceptibility of a block of side $L^n$ at criticality grows like its side to the power $\gamma/\nu$. This is the form in which the exponents are measured numerically, and it requires no infinite-volume limit.

    Corrections to scaling. The approach to a fixed point along an irrelevant direction with eigenvalue $\lambda_2$, $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert<1$, modifies each power law by a factor $1+O(L^{-n(1-\log\lvert\lambda_2\rvert/\log L)})$. A complex pair of eigenvalues produces an oscillating factor, which is what distinguishes the hierarchical model's subleading behaviour from a pure power law and is analysed at the nontrivial fixed point.

    Two independent exponents. The dictionary uses exactly two inputs, $\kappa$ and $\nu$; a model with a fixed point but no hyperscaling has two independent exponents and the other three relations fail, as in percolation for $\alpha<d/3$. Five exponents cannot be independent in a model with a one-parameter family of fixed points, which is the content of the scaling hypothesis in its general form.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    The reading of the indices off the linearised renormalisation map is the content of Bleher and Sinai's second paper on the hierarchical model [@bleher-sinai-1975], where the indices are computed as functions of the spectrum at the fixed point. The classical long-range values are those of Fisher, Ma and Nickel [@fisher-ma-nickel-1972], and the rigorous mean-field region for long-range Ising models is due to Aizenman and Fernandez [@aizenman-fernandez-1988]. The two-point exponent for hierarchical percolation and the volume-exponent obstruction are Hutchcroft's [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021].
