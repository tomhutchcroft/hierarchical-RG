    Correction exponent of a subleading eigenvalue

    definition · C6VW · in Corrections to scaling

    For an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $D\mathcal R$ with $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$, the correction exponent is

    $$\omega(\lambda)=-\frac{\log\lvert\lambda\rvert}{\log L},$$

    a positive number; for a complex eigenvalue $\lambda=\lvert\lambda\rvert e^{i\varphi}$ the pair contributes the same power together with the phase $\varphi/\log L$ per unit of $\log\xi$. The correction exponent of the model is $\omega=\omega(\lambda_2)$ for the subleading eigenvalue.

    The exponent measures the length scale at which a perturbation decays by a factor $e$: after $n$ scales a perturbation of size $\epsilon$ in the direction $\rho_2$ has size $\epsilon L^{-\omega n}=\epsilon\xi^{-\omega}$ when $L^n$ is the correlation length.
