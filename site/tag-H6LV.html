    Radial two-point functions, exchangeable block sums, and conditional expectations on a child

    corollary · H6LV · in The isometries of the hierarchical lattice

    Let the kernel depend only on the level, and let the model be the hierarchical spin system on a block with free boundary.

    (i) Radiality. For the model on $\mathbb H^d_L$, the two-point function satisfies $\tau(x,y)=\tau(0,y-x)$ and depends only on $\operatorname{lev}(y-x)$.

    (ii) Exchangeability of child sums. In the model on $\Lambda_N$, the $m$-tuple of block sums $(S_a)$ of the children of $\Lambda_N$ is exchangeable, and so is the corresponding tuple for the children of any block.

    (iii) Constancy on a child. For $x,y$ in the same child of a block of level $n$, and for any function $F$ of the child sums, $\mathbb E[\sigma_xF]=\mathbb E[\sigma_yF]$ and $\mathbb E[\sigma_x\mid(S_a)]$ takes the same value at $x$ and $y$.

    Proof

    An isometry preserving the level of every difference preserves the Hamiltonian, because the Hamiltonian is a sum of couplings that depend only on the level; and it preserves the prior, which is a product. Hence it preserves the Gibbs measure, and expectations of functions of the configuration composed with it are unchanged. This is the mechanism of all three statements.

    (i) Given $x,y$ with $z=y-x$ of level $n$, the theorem gives an isometry fixing $0$ and sending $z$ to any prescribed vertex of level $n$; the pair $(x,y)$ is sent to $(0,w)$ with $w$ of level $n$, and $\tau$ is preserved. So $\tau(x,y)$ depends only on $n$ and equals $\tau(0,w)$ for every $w$ at that level.

    (ii) Translation by any $v\in\Lambda_N$ preserves $\Lambda_N$, preserves the Hamiltonian (levels are translation invariant) and permutes the children, since the children are the cosets of $\Lambda_{N-1}$ and $v+\cdot$ is a bijection of the set of cosets. For any two children $B,B'$ choose $v\in B-B'$; then the translation by $v$ maps $B'$ to $B$ and permutes the others, and it preserves the law. Iterating gives exchangeability of the tuple.

    (iii) Translation by any $v\in\Lambda_{n-1}$ maps each child of a level-$n$ block to itself — the children are cosets of $\Lambda_{n-1}$ — so it fixes every child sum, and it is an isometry, so it preserves the law. Any two vertices of a child differ by an element of $\Lambda_{n-1}$, so the transitivity of these translations inside the child gives the first identity; the second follows by taking $F$ to run over the generating functions of the conditional law, or directly from the definition of conditional expectation.

    Statement (iii) is exactly what licenses the step "$\mathbb E[\sigma_x\mid(S_a)]=S_a/\lvert\Lambda_{n-1}\rvert$" in the Gaussian two-point computation, and its analogues elsewhere. Note that it is a statement about conditional expectations given the sums, not about the conditional law being uniform: the conditional law of the spins in a child given their sum is not uniform, but its first moment is.
