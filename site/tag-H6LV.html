    Radial two-point functions, exchangeable block sums, and conditional expectations on a child

    corollary · H6LV · in The isometries of the hierarchical lattice

    Let the kernel depend only on the level, and let the model be the hierarchical spin system on a block with free boundary.

    (i) Radiality. For the model on $\mathbb H^d_L$, the two-point function satisfies $\tau(x,y)=\tau(0,y-x)$ and depends only on $\operatorname{lev}(y-x)$.

    (ii) Exchangeability of child sums. In the model on $\Lambda_N$, the $m$-tuple of block sums $(S_a)$ of the children of $\Lambda_N$ is exchangeable, and so is the corresponding tuple for the children of any block.

    (iii) Constancy on a child. For $x,y$ in the same child of a block of level $n$, and for any function $F$ of the child sums, $\mathbb E[\sigma_xF]=\mathbb E[\sigma_yF]$ and $\mathbb E[\sigma_x\mid(S_a)]$ takes the same value at $x$ and $y$.

    Proof

    An isometry preserving the level of every difference preserves the Hamiltonian, because the Hamiltonian is a sum of couplings that depend only on the level; and it preserves the prior, which is a product. Hence it preserves the Gibbs measure, and expectations of functions of the configuration composed with it are unchanged. This is the mechanism of all three statements.

    (i) Given $x,y$ with $z=y-x$ of level $n$, the theorem gives an isometry fixing $0$ and sending $z$ to any prescribed vertex of level $n$; the pair $(x,y)$ is sent to $(0,w)$ with $w$ of level $n$, and $\tau$ is preserved. So $\tau(x,y)$ depends only on $n$ and equals $\tau(0,w)$ for every $w$ at that level.

    (ii) Translation by any $v\in\Lambda_N$ preserves $\Lambda_N$, preserves the Hamiltonian (levels are translation invariant) and permutes the children, since the children are the cosets of $\Lambda_{N-1}$ and $v+\cdot$ is a bijection of the set of cosets. For any two children $B,B'$ choose $v\in B-B'$; then the translation by $v$ maps $B'$ to $B$ and permutes the others, and it preserves the law. Iterating gives exchangeability of the tuple.

    (iii) Translation by any $v\in\Lambda_{n-1}$ maps each child of a level-$n$ block to itself — the children are cosets of $\Lambda_{n-1}$ — so it fixes every child sum, and it is an isometry, so it preserves the law. Any two vertices of a child differ by an element of $\Lambda_{n-1}$, so the transitivity of these translations inside the child gives the first identity; the second follows by taking $F$ to run over the generating functions of the conditional law, or directly from the definition of conditional expectation.

    Statement (iii) is exactly what licenses the step "$\mathbb E[\sigma_x\mid(S_a)]=S_a/\lvert\Lambda_{n-1}\rvert$" in the Gaussian two-point computation, and its analogues elsewhere. Note that it is a statement about conditional expectations given the sums, not about the conditional law being uniform: the conditional law of the spins in a child given their sum is not uniform, but its first moment is.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    Translations alone are not enough. A translation preserves the displacement $y-x$, so it moves a pair only within its own class and cannot relate two different pairs at the same level. The coordinate-wise bijections supply the rest, and they are generally not translations: in $\Lambda_2$ with $L=3$, $d=1$, the map sending $(x_1,x_2)$ to $(2x_1,x_2)$ is a coordinate-wise bijection fixing $0$, and it is not a translation, since it exchanges $(1,0)$ and $(2,0)$ while a translation fixing $0$ is the identity.

    A level-preserving bijection that is not an isometry. Take $d=1$, $L=2$ and the block $\Lambda_3$ of eight vertices, written as triples. Transpose $(0,1,1)$ and $(1,0,1)$ and fix everything else. Both moved vertices have level three, and every other vertex is fixed, so the level of every vertex is preserved. But the pair $\{(0,0,1),(0,1,1)\}$ has level two, being a pair differing in coordinate two, while its image $\{(0,0,1),(1,0,1)\}$ has level one. The map therefore changes a level of a pair and is not an isometry.

    The same symmetry on another block system. The theorem is stated for the lattice; on a general ultrametric block system of the general article the translation part survives verbatim, since levels are translation invariant there too, and transitivity on pairs at a level holds whenever the coefficient group is large enough to carry the coordinate-wise bijections.

    The p-adic case. On $\mathbb Z_p$ the maps $x\mapsto a+bx$ with $a\in\mathbb Z_p$ and $\lvert b\rvert_p=1$ are isometries, since $\lvert b(x-y)\rvert_p=\lvert x-y\rvert_p$, and they are already transitive on pairs at a level: if $\lvert x-y\rvert_p=\lvert x'-y'\rvert_p$ then $b=(x'-y')/(x-y)$ has $\lvert b\rvert_p=1$ and $a=x'-bx$ lies in $\mathbb Z_p$. So the conclusion of the theorem holds there with a smaller group.

    The two families are a subgroup, not the whole group. The isometry group is larger. Permuting the $L^d$ children of a single block and leaving every other vertex alone is an isometry, since it moves each child of that block onto a child of the same block and preserves the internal structure of each; and such a map need not be a composition of a translation with coordinate-wise bijections. What cannot be done is to permute the coordinates: the level records which coordinate is the outermost, so in $\Lambda_2$ with $L=2$, $d=2$ the vertices $0$ and $((0,0),(1,0))$ are at level two, while their images under the interchange of the two coordinates are at level one. The two families are used below because they are the ones that act transitively on pairs, not because they exhaust $\operatorname{Isom}$.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    Isometries of a general block system. For a block system $(G,G_n)$ the same two families exist whenever $G_n/G_{n+1}$ carries enough bijections; the translation part always does, and the conclusion (i) of the corollary then holds for radial kernels with no further hypothesis.

    Vector spins and percolation. The isometries act on configurations by permuting vertices, so they preserve vector-valued models and percolation configurations as well. This is why the two-point function of a radial kernel is radial in those models, and why the sibling clusters in the percolation recursion are equidistributed.

    Symmetry breaking. Below the critical temperature the Gibbs measure need not be invariant under the full isometry group; the symmetric state is the average over the broken ones. The statements above are about the symmetric Gibbs measure, which is the one the free-boundary finite-volume models converge to along symmetric subsequences.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    The level isometries are used by Hutchcroft to convert an averaged two-point bound into a pointwise one, on the grounds that any two pairs at a distance are exchanged by an isometry preserving the law [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021]; the proof given here is the elementary form of that observation. The symmetry of the block sums is used from the beginning of the subject, in the reduction of the recursion to a single variable [@bleher-sinai-1973], and again in the p-adic setting [@abdesselam-2018]. The counterexample in the examples section is included because the distinction between preserving vertex levels and preserving pair levels is easy to miss.
