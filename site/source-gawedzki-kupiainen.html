    Gawedzki and Kupiainen: the local analysis of a non-Gaussian fixed point

    reading map · source-gawedzki-kupiainen

    This is the reading map for the five papers in which Krzysztof Gawedzki and Antti Kupiainen built the local theory of the nontrivial fixed point of the hierarchical renormalisation map. The group is more varied than Koch and Wittwer's, and its role in this wiki is specific: it is where the neighbourhood of the fixed point is analysed — the expansion of the exponents in the distance from the marginal kernel exponent, the unstable manifold, and the convergence of rescaled correlations — as opposed to the questions of existence, which belong to Bleher and Sinai and to Koch and Wittwer.

    Two parameters organise the group, and both are small in the papers that use them: the departure ε = d/2 - α of the kernel exponent from the marginal value at which the quartic coupling becomes relevant, and the inverse number of components 1/N of the spin. Neither is a small parameter of the model. The fixed point exists for every α in (0,d), but the analysis of it is local, and the hierarchical approximation is what makes the local analysis into a theorem rather than a formal series.

    The group is also the reason the wiki distinguishes the marginal exponent α = d/2 from the others: the expansion in ε is an expansion around that value, so the threshold announced in the exponent dictionary and computed in the spectrum article is the point at which these papers expand.

    What is taken from each

    The 1983 paper

    This is the paper the wiki cites for the linear theory of the nontrivial fixed point, and the citation is narrow on purpose. Bleher and Sinai prove that a nontrivial fixed point exists, that the critical orbit converges to it, and that the correlation-length exponent is read off the linearisation; what they do not give is the value of the leading eigenvalue away from the marginal exponent. The 1983 paper is where that is computed, by expanding in the distance from the marginal exponent: the fixed point is close to the Gaussian one, the correction to the multiplier is of first order in ε = d/2 - α, and the resulting value of ν is not the classical 1/α.

    The mechanism, in the reviewer's summary, is worth recording because it is the model for every local analysis in the field: find a small set of laws which the transformation maps into itself except along one direction, and show that inside it two successive iterates move closer together at the critical coupling. The one direction that is not contracted is the relevant one, and it is what produces the second exponent. Everything the wiki says about the spectrum at the nontrivial fixed point is an instance of this statement with the eigenvalues made explicit.

    The 1984 paper

    Its subject is the consequence the exponent dictionary needs: not the fixed point but the field it generates. If the critical orbit converges after rescaling, the correlations of the model converge to those of a limit law, and the two-point exponent η = 2 - α is the rate at which the rescaled block spin's mass is spread. The 1984 paper is where the convergence of the rescaled correlations is proved in the hierarchical approximation, and the wiki cites it jointly with the 1983 paper for that statement rather than separating the two.

    The 1980 paper and the 1982 paper

    The 1980 paper is the general programme: a block-spin expansion for massless lattice theories that converges, with the hierarchical model as the example in which the expansion can be completed. For this wiki it is the honest statement of where the exactness ends — the Euclidean expansion has the same shape, but the geometry of the blocks changes with scale and the expansion is no longer an identity. The 1982 paper is the triviality discussion: the hierarchy model is used to argue that phi-fourth in four dimensions is trivial, and it is also where the analyticity technique appears that Koch and Wittwer later replace. Neither is cited for a theorem the wiki uses; they are cited for the programme and for the method.

    The 1984 paper with Tirozzi

    The gradient model differs from the models of the rest of this wiki in that the interaction acts on differences of the field, so the large-field region behaves differently and the perturbation series can be resummed in the Borel sense. The paper proves that the series for the dielectric constant and the free energy density are Borel summable. It matters here because it is the technique Koch and Wittwer import into the non-gradient case, which is what allows them to prove existence without a small parameter.

    What the group does not do

    Three boundaries are worth stating, because the wiki relies on them.

    Existence is not the subject. The fixed point is assumed or constructed locally; the global existence theorem for the hierarchical model is Bleher and Sinai's, and the parameter-free construction for the three-dimensional scalar case is Koch and Wittwer's, as that reading map records.

    The analysis is local. The results are for ε small or N large. They are not statements about the three-dimensional Ising model at the physical value of the parameters, and the wiki does not use them as such; where an exponent is quoted away from the neighbourhood, it is quoted as a conjecture.

    The setting is the hierarchical approximation. The 1980 paper is explicit that the Euclidean expansion is the target and the hierarchical model the place where it is completed. The Euclidean comparison says what fails when the hierarchy is dropped, and no paper in this group closes that.

    How to read the group

    For a reader who wants the exponents, the order is 1983 then 1984: the first gives the multiplier and hence ν, the second the scaling limit and hence η. For a reader who wants the method, read the 1983 paper after the statement of the Bleher-Sinai theorem, because the local analysis is the linearisation of a theorem that is already known to hold. The 1980 and 1982 papers are for the context in which the hierarchical model is a step towards the Euclidean theory, and the Tirozzi paper belongs with the Koch-Wittwer method rather than with the rest.
