    The free energy and its singularity

    The recursion summed scale by scale, the Gaussian case in closed form, and where the curvature diverges.

    theorem · part 2 · 6 sections · 3 tagged assertions · hierarchical-free-energy

    The decimation article proves that the free energy per site converges, $F=\lim_nF_n$, and that it is convex in the intensity. It does not say what $F$ is, or what happens to it at the critical intensity, and those are the two questions this article answers for the one case in which they can be answered by computation.

    The mechanism is the per-scale decomposition. Each decimation step contributes one term to $F$, the logarithm of an expectation taken over $m$ independent copies of the block spin one scale down; for a Gaussian prior that expectation is an integral of a quadratic form, and for a Gaussian vector the sum of the components and the sum of their squares are independent. So the term is a product of two one-dimensional Gaussian integrals, and the whole free energy is an explicit series. Nothing in the Ising case is elementary, and the last section says what survives there.

    The per-scale terms#

    Let $\varrho=N(0,1)$, $b_k=\beta J_k$, and $v_n$ the variance of the block spin of $\Lambda_n$, which is $v_n=m^n/(1-\beta C_n)$ with $C_n=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n\setminus0}J(\lVert x\rVert)$. Then

    $$F=\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{1}{m^k}\Bigl[-\tfrac12\log\bigl(1-b_k(m-1)v_{k-1}\bigr)-\tfrac{m-1}{2}\log\bigl(1+b_kv_{k-1}\bigr)\Bigr],$$

    and the series converges uniformly on $0\leq\beta\leq\beta_c^{\mathrm G}=1/C$. Consequently $F$ is continuous on that interval and real analytic in its interior — while its second derivative diverges as $\beta\uparrow\beta_c$, which is where the transition is.

    Proof

    By the free energy recursion, the level-$k$ increment of $F$ is $m^{-k}$ times the logarithm of $\mathbb E[\exp(b_k\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'})]$ over $m$ independent copies of $\mu_{k-1}$, which for the Gaussian prior is $N(0,v_{k-1})$. The exponent is the quadratic form of the matrix that is $\tfrac12$ off the diagonal and $0$ on it, whose eigenvalues are $\tfrac{m-1}{2}$ along the diagonal direction and $-\tfrac12$ on its orthogonal complement. In an orthonormal eigenbasis the coordinates are independent $N(0,v_{k-1})$, so the expectation factorises into $m$ one-dimensional Gaussian integrals, one with coefficient $b_k(m-1)$ and $m-1$ with coefficient $-b_k$:

    $$\bigl(1-b_k(m-1)v_{k-1}\bigr)^{-1/2}\bigl(1+b_kv_{k-1}\bigr)^{-(m-1)/2}.$$

    The variance $v_{k-1}=m^{k-1}/(1-\beta C_{k-1})$ is the closed form of the Gaussian article. For convergence, put $\varepsilon=\beta_c-\beta\geq0$. For $k$ large enough that $\beta C_{k-1}$ is within $\varepsilon C$ of one, $v_{k-1}\asymp m^{k-1}/(\varepsilon C)$ and hence

    $$b_k(m-1)v_{k-1}\asymp\frac{(m-1)\beta}{L^d\,C\,\varepsilon}\,L^{-\alpha k},$$

    which is $O(L^{-\alpha k}/\varepsilon)$ and so bounded by a constant depending on $\varepsilon$ but not on $k$; the second factor's argument lies between one and two. Hence each term is at most $m^{-k}$ times a constant, uniformly in $k$ and in $\beta\in[0,\beta_c]$, which gives the uniform convergence, the continuity of $F$ and its analyticity in the interior. The constant degenerates as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, and that degeneration is what the divergence of the second derivative measures.

    Two features of the display are worth naming. The first factor is the one the variance recursion knows about: its argument is $1-b_k(m-1)v_{k-1}$, whose vanishing is exactly the blow-up of the variance recursion, so the free energy integral and the recursion fail at the same place. The second factor is the price of summing over the block's internal freedom: it comes from the $m-1$ directions of the tilt that are not the diagonal one, which the variance does not see, and without it the free energy would be a sum of the terms the recursion controls.

    The singularity#

    The series is explicit, so its singularity can be located exactly in the variable that matters, the distance to the critical intensity.

    The figure is the statement in one picture. $F$ increases and remains finite up to the critical intensity, which is what the uniform convergence gives; the curvature increases without bound, so $F$ is continuously differentiable but not twice so, and the transition is second order. Neither feature is special to the Gaussian case: what is special is that here the two can be computed.

    The exponent can be read off the same series by a scaling argument, and it is worth recording because it is the dictionary's hyperscaling relation derived rather than assumed. Write $\varepsilon=\beta_c-\beta$. Differentiating the $k$-th term twice in $\beta$ brings down a factor $\varepsilon^{-2}$ from its dependence through $1-\beta C_{k-1}$, for the scales at which that denominator is of order $\varepsilon$; those are the scales with $L^{-\alpha k}\asymp\varepsilon$, and each carries the weight $m^{-k}\asymp\varepsilon^{d/\alpha}$. All other scales contribute less, so

    $$F''(\beta)\ \asymp\ \varepsilon^{d/\alpha}\cdot\varepsilon^{-2}=\varepsilon^{-(2-d/\alpha)},$$

    so the heat capacity $C_V=\beta^2F''$ diverges like $\varepsilon^{-(2-d/\alpha)}$ — that is, the specific-heat exponent is $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d/\alpha$, which is what the dictionary obtains from hyperscaling once $\nu=1/\alpha$. At $d/\alpha=2$, which for $d=1$ is $\alpha=1/2$, the exponent vanishes and the divergence is logarithmic; the finite sum plotted above cannot distinguish a logarithm from a small power, and the argument is what distinguishes them. This derivation is a scaling argument, not a proof: it identifies the terms that dominate and their weight, and a proof would need the sum bounded above and below uniformly in $\varepsilon$, which is not done here.

    What survives for Ising spins#

    Nothing in the derivation is available for the Ising prior. The per-scale expectation is an average over $m$ integer-valued spins, it is not a Gaussian integral, and its logarithm is not elementary; the recursion for the law of the block spin is the only access, and it is infinite-dimensional. What survives is everything that did not use the closed form: the free energy exists and is convex and continuous, the per-scale decomposition holds with the expectation written as it stands, and the location of the singularity is the intensity at which the orbit leaves the domain of attraction of the trivial fixed point — which is the trichotomy, not a computation.

    So the honest summary of the specific heat for the Ising model in this wiki is: the dictionary's relation $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d\nu$ holds if hyperscaling does, the value of $\nu$ is known only through the linearisation at the nontrivial fixed point, and the free energy's own curvature is not computed anywhere. The Gaussian case is the one in which the three statements are a computation, and that is why it is recorded separately rather than as a remark in the exponent article.

    The Ising case, computed as far as it goes#

    The Gaussian series is elementary because the per-scale expectation is a Gaussian integral. For Ising spins it is a sum over $m$ integers, and it is not elementary — but it is computable, by carrying the law of the block spin from scale to scale. The identity is the same one the Gaussian computation used, with the tilt split through the matrix structure: with $b=b_k$ and $m$ children,

    $$\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'}=\frac{m-1}{2}\,y_1^2-\frac12\sum_{i\geq2}y_i^2$$

    in an orthonormal eigenbasis, so the tilted weight factors into a function of the total $S=\sum_as_a$ and a product of one-site weights. For $m=2$ that makes one decimation step a convolution: if $\mu$ is the law of the block spin one scale down, then the unnormalised next law is

    $$\mu'(s)\propto e^{bs^2/2}\bigl(\tilde\mu*\tilde\mu\bigr)(s),\qquad \tilde\mu(x)=e^{-bx^2/2}\mu(x),$$

    and the level-$k$ term of the free energy is $m^{-k}$ times the logarithm of the normalising constant. The convolution is done by fast Fourier transform and the whole computation in the logarithmic domain, so that neither the tilt nor its inverse overflows.

    The recursion reproduces the exact finite-volume free energy: for $n\leq4$ its output agrees with direct enumeration of the block-spin laws to twelve decimal places. Since every per-scale term is nonnegative — the tilted expectation is at least one, as the tilt has nonnegative mean — the sequence $F_n$ increases to $F$, and each computed $F_n$ is a certified lower bound for the free energy at that intensity:

    The table is honest about its limits, which are the interesting part. Away from criticality the sequence has converged by $n=12$: the first row does not move between twelve and sixteen scales and moves in the fourth decimal at twenty. Towards the critical intensity it does not converge at that size at all — the entry at $0.80$ is still rising in the fourth decimal at $n=20$ — and the reason is structural rather than numerical. Near criticality the per-scale contribution $m^{-k}\log Z_k$ decays only like $k\,m^{-k}$, because $\log Z_k$ grows linearly in the scale, so reaching a fixed accuracy needs a number of scales comparable to the logarithm of the reciprocal; and each scale doubles the support of the law, so the method's memory grows like $2^{2n}$. Twenty scales is what fits comfortably, and twenty is not enough.

    What follows for the questions this article is about is negative and worth stating plainly. The free energy itself is computable to a few digits and certified from below; its first derivative is not, because differentiating a truncated sequence near criticality amplifies the truncation error, which changes with the intensity faster than the derivative does; and its second derivative, the heat capacity, is further out of reach. So the curvature of the Ising free energy is not computed in this wiki, and the specific-heat exponent for the Ising model is not measured here. The Gaussian case is the one in which the series is elementary, and the reason it is recorded separately is exactly that the Ising case resists this computation for a reason one can write down.

    The energy, and a trap#

    The heat capacity is the derivative of the energy, and the energy has an exact form in this model that is worth knowing even though it does not rescue the Ising computation. Write $A_j$ for the sum of the squares of the block sums over the level-$j$ blocks inside $\Lambda_n$.

    For every configuration,

    $$H_{\Lambda_n}=-\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_k\bigl(A_k-A_{k-1}\bigr),\qquad A_j=\sum_{\text{level-}j\text{ blocks }B\subset\Lambda_n}\Bigl(\sum_{x\in B}\sigma_x\Bigr)^2.$$
    Proof

    The pairs of a level-$k$ block $B$ fall into two classes: those inside one of its children, which have level below $k$, and those in two distinct children, which all have level exactly $k$ by the child decomposition. Hence the sum of $\sigma_x\sigma_y$ over the pairs of $B$ at its own level is $\tfrac12\bigl(S_B^2-\sum_aS_{B_a}^2\bigr)$, and summing over the level-$k$ blocks makes the second term the level-$(k-1)$ sum. Adding over $k$ gives the display.

    The identity is exact configuration by configuration, and it is the reason the energy of a hierarchical model is a local object: it is a functional of the squared block sums at consecutive scales, which is what one expects from a model whose interaction is organised by scale.

    The trap is the next step, which looks automatic and is wrong. Since the level-$j$ blocks are translates of one another inside $\Lambda_n$, they have equal expected squared sums, so $\mathbb E[A_j]=m^{n-j}W_j$ for a single number $W_j$ — and the temptation is to take $W_j$ to be the variance $V_j$ of the free block spin of $\Lambda_j$, which is what the recursion computes. That would give the energy from the variance sequence alone. It is false: a level-$j$ block sitting inside $\Lambda_n$ is coupled to its siblings, so its marginal law is not the free measure on $\Lambda_j$ but the Gibbs measure with a sibling environment. The discrepancy is small and not negligible: at $\beta=0.3$, $n=2$ and $m=2$ the energy density computed from the free variances is $-0.024604$ where the enumerated value is $-0.024955$, a relative error of $1.4\%$. The check that guards this article verifies both halves — the identity exactly over every configuration, and the size of the gap — because the identity is true and the step from it to the free variances is the one that fails.

    So the energy is computable and its expression is elementary, but not through the variance sequence: computing it needs the joint law of the block sums of the children, which is what the convolution recursion carries and what makes the cost grow with the scale. That is the same wall the heat capacity meets from the other side, and it is why the Ising curvature is not computed in this wiki.

    Variants and generalisations#

    Other priors with a closed form. The derivation used only that the prior is Gaussian, so that $S$ and $Q$ are independent. For a prior that is a mixture of Gaussians the same computation gives a sum over the mixture, and the per-scale term becomes a finite sum of logarithms; for a prior with a quartic term the integral is not elementary and the series in $\beta$ is only asymptotic, which is where the large-field problem begins.

    An external field. A field adds a linear term to the tilt, the Gaussian integral still closes, and the per-scale term gains a factor depending on the field and on the block spin's mean; the free energy then has a singularity along the whole critical curve rather than at a point. The wiki does not develop this, and the reason is that the field's recursion in the map article is a separate argument.
