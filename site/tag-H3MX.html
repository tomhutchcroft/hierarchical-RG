    Exponents of the Gaussian fixed point

    corollary · H3MX · in The Gaussian renormalisation group

    The Gaussian fixed point has

    $$\nu=\frac{1}{\alpha},\qquad \eta=2-\alpha,\qquad \gamma=1,\qquad \beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\frac{d-\alpha}{2\alpha},\qquad \delta=\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha},\qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-\frac{d}{\alpha}.$$
    Proof of the exponent values

    The multiplier at the fixed point is $\lambda_1=L^\alpha$, so $\nu=\log L/\log\lambda_1=1/\alpha$ by the definition of $\nu$ in the dictionary. The normalisation of the recursion is at $\theta_0=\tfrac12(d+\alpha)$, and the critical orbit has $v_n=L^{2\theta_0n}$ by the previous section, so the susceptibility of a block grows like $L^{\alpha n}$ and $\kappa=\alpha$, that is $\eta=2-\alpha$. The remaining values follow from the dictionary with $\kappa=\alpha$ and $\nu=1/\alpha$: $\gamma=\kappa\nu=1$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)=\frac{d-\alpha}{2\alpha}$, $\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa}=\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d\nu=2-d/\alpha$, and the susceptibility exponent is checked directly by the closed form $\chi=\beta_c(\beta_c-\beta)^{-1}$.

    The values are those of the classical long-range theory, with $\nu=1/\alpha$ independent of the dimension and $\eta$ tending to $1$ as $\alpha\uparrow d$. They satisfy the scaling relations $\gamma=\nu(2-\eta)$, $\gamma=\beta_{\mathrm{o}}(\delta-1)$ and $2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=d\nu$ identically in $\alpha$ and $d$; the second is $\frac{d-\alpha}{2\alpha}\cdot\frac{2\alpha}{d-\alpha}=1$, and the third is $2-(2-d/\alpha)=d/\alpha$. At $\alpha=d/2$ they reduce to $\nu=2/d$, $\eta=2-d/2$, $\delta=3$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=1/2$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=0$: the Ising values of $\delta$, $\gamma$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}$, with $\nu=2/d$ in place of the short-range mean-field value $\tfrac12$, the difference being the hyperscaling relation $2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=d\nu$, which holds here with $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=0$ and fails for the short-range mean-field exponents above $d=4$.
