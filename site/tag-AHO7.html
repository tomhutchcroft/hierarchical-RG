    The coupling of a block to its complement, and the volume it subtends

    proposition · AHO7 · in The infinite-volume limit

    Let $n\geq0$ and $x\in\Lambda_n$. Then

    $$B_n:=\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_n}\lvert J(x-y)\rvert=J(1-L^{-d})\frac{L^{-\alpha(n+1)}}{1-L^{-\alpha}},$$

    independently of $x\in\Lambda_n$, and in particular $B_n\to0$. The total coupling between $\Lambda_n$ and its complement is $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$, and

    $$\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n=\frac{J(1-L^{-d})L^{-\alpha}}{1-L^{-\alpha}}\,L^{(d-\alpha)n}\ \longrightarrow\ \begin{cases}0,&\alpha>d,\\ \infty,&\alpha
    Proof

    Let $x\in\Lambda_n$ and $y\notin\Lambda_n$, so that $x_i=0$ for $i>n$ and $y$ has some coordinate above $n$ different from $0$. Then $x-y$ vanishes above the largest index $m$ with $y_m\neq0$, and does not vanish there, so $\operatorname{lev}(x-y)=\operatorname{lev}(y)$: the level of the pair is the level of $y$ as a vertex, exactly when $x$ is in the block below $y$. Hence $J(x-y)=J_{\operatorname{lev}(y)}$, and summing over the shell at level $j$ gives

    $$B_n=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\bigl(L^{dj}-L^{d(j-1)}\bigr)JL^{-(d+\alpha)j}=J(1-L^{-d})\sum_{j\geq n+1}L^{-\alpha j}=\frac{J(1-L^{-d})L^{-\alpha(n+1)}}{1-L^{-\alpha}},$$

    using the shell counts of the lattice article and the geometric series, which converges because $\alpha>0$. The total coupling is $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ with $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert=L^{dn}$, and $L^{dn}L^{-\alpha(n+1)}=L^{-\alpha}L^{(d-\alpha)n}$ gives the dichotomy.

    Both statements are about the same number, and they pull in opposite directions. Per vertex the boundary is negligible for every $\alpha>0$: that is the hypothesis the Gibbs property needs, and it holds in the whole range of the model. The block totals it, and the cross energy $-\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n,y\notin\Lambda_n}J(x-y)\sigma_x\sigma_y$ has modulus at most $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ with each pair counted once from the block's side, so $\beta\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ — the largest exponent the boundary can contribute — diverges exactly when $\alpha<d$. So in the long-range range the boundary of a block carries unbounded energy while no single vertex feels more than $B_n$, and that is the precise sense in which the model is long-range: not that a spin is strongly coupled to the outside, but that there are too many outside spins. For $\alpha>d$ the cross energy stays bounded, and the boundary is a finite-volume effect.

    Neither case obstructs the limit. The next two sections take the limit with only $B_n\to0$, and the dichotomy is recorded because it is what distinguishes the two ranges of the model, and because the marginal value $\alpha=d$ — the value at which the cross energy neither vanishes nor diverges — is where the model's own criteria become delicate, as the vector article records.

    ## The conditional law is continuous in the boundary {#section-continuity}

    Everything below is a consequence of one Lipschitz estimate, which says that changing the boundary field changes the conditional law of a finite region by at most the size of the change.
