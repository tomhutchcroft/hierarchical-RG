    The critical intensity is the reciprocal of the operator norm

    proposition · B5WC · in The Gaussian renormalisation group

    Let $A$ be convolution by $J(\lVert\cdot\rVert)$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb H^d_L)$ and $A_n$ its compression to $\Lambda_n$. Then $\lVert A\rVert=\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)$, and $\lambda_{\max}(A_n)\to\lVert A\rVert$ as $n\to\infty$.

    Proof that the norm is the row sum

    For $\phi\in\ell^2$ with finite support, $\langle\phi,A\phi\rangle=\sum_{x,y}\phi_xJ(x-y)\phi_y\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_2^2\sup_x\sum_yJ(x-y)=\lVert\phi\rVert_2^2\sum_{z\neq0}J(z)$ by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the pairs $(x,y)$ with weight $J$. For the reverse inequality take $\phi=\mathbf 1_{\Lambda_n}/\sqrt{\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert}$, which has norm one: then $\langle\phi,A\phi\rangle=\sum_zJ(z)\lvert\Lambda_n\cap(\Lambda_n-z)\rvert/\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert$. For fixed $z$ the ratio tends to $1$ as $n\to\infty$, and it is at most $1$, so dominated convergence gives convergence to $\sum_zJ(z)$. Hence $\lVert A_n\rVert\to\lVert A\rVert$, and $\lVert A\rVert$ is at least the limit of quotients and at most $\sum_zJ(z)$.

    For the finite-volume model, the Gaussian measure with density $\exp(-\frac\beta2\sigma^TA_n\sigma)$ exists exactly when $I-\beta A_n$ is positive definite, that is $\beta<1/\lambda_{\max}(A_n)$, and the critical intensity of the infinite lattice is the limit. This agrees with the recursion, and it shows that the two-sided role of $\alpha>0$ is unavoidable: at $\alpha\leq0$ the row sum diverges and there is no finite intensity at which the field exists.
