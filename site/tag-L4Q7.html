    Level, distance and hierarchical balls

    definition · L4Q7 · in The hierarchical lattice

    For $x\neq0$ put $\operatorname{lev}(x)=\max\{i\geq1:x_i\neq0\}$ and $\operatorname{lev}(0)=0$, and define the hierarchical distance

    $$\lVert x\rVert=L^{\operatorname{lev}(x)}\quad(x\neq0),\qquad \lVert0\rVert=0,$$

    and $\lVert x-y\rVert$ for a pair of vertices. For $n\geq0$ the level-$n$ block at the origin is $\Lambda_n=\{x:\operatorname{lev}(x)\leq n\}$, the set of sequences vanishing above coordinate $n$, and the level-$n$ block at $x$ is the coset $x+\Lambda_n$. A vertex of level $n$ is one at distance $L^n$ from the origin; the shell at level $n$ is $\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}$.

    Here $\Lambda_0=\{0\}$, and the first nontrivial displacement available on $\mathbb H^d_L$ has length $L$. The notation $\langle x\rangle=\max\{1,\lVert x\rVert\}$ regularises the distance at the origin and is used whenever a formula has to be read at $x=0$.
