    The decimation operator

    definition · R1FB · in The renormalisation group map

    $$\mathcal T_b\mu(A')=\frac{1}{Z_b(\mu)}\int_{\mathbb R^m}\mathbf 1_{A'}\Bigl(\sum_{a\in A}s_a\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(b\sum_{a

    for Borel $A'\subseteq\mathbb R$, and the scaled decimation operator at exponent $\theta>0$ is

    $$\mathcal R_{\theta,b}\mu=\bigl(s\mapsto L^{-\theta}s\bigr)_*\bigl(\mathcal T_b\mu\bigr),$$

    the image of $\mathcal T_b\mu$ under multiplication by $L^{-\theta}$. The quadratic form of the tilt is $\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'}=\tfrac12\bigl(\bigl(\sum_as_a\bigr)^2-\sum_as_a^2\bigr)$, so the tilt rewards configurations whose sum is large in absolute value.

    If $\mu$ is supported in $[-K,K]$ then $0<Z_b(\mu)<\infty$ for every $b\geq0$, because the exponent is bounded on $[-K,K]^m$; and $\mathcal T_b\mu$ is then supported in $[-mK,mK]$. For a law with tails heavier than Gaussian the normaliser can diverge, and then the operator is undefined at that $b$; the Gaussian case in the next article locates the threshold exactly.

    The full context, including the running hypotheses and the proof, is in The renormalisation group map. To cite this assertion, link to tag-R1FB.html or to the article anchor directly.
