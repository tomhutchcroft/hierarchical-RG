    Fixed point, relevant direction and eigenvalue

    definition · V2HD · in The renormalisation group map

    A law $\nu^*$ is a fixed point of $\mathcal R_{\theta,b}$ if $\mathcal R_{\theta,b}\nu^*=\nu^*$ and $0<\int s^2d\nu^*<\infty$. At a fixed point, a bounded linear operator $D\mathcal R$ on the space of signed measures of zero total mass is the linearisation if

    $$\frac{\mathcal R_{\theta,b}\bigl((1-\epsilon)\nu^*+\epsilon\rho\bigr)-\nu^*}{\epsilon}\longrightarrow D\mathcal R\,\rho\quad(\epsilon\downarrow0)$$

    for every signed measure $\rho$ of zero mass with $\int\lvert s\rvert^2d\lvert\rho\rvert(s)<\infty$. A direction is an eigenmeasure $D\mathcal R\rho=\lambda\rho$; it is relevant if $\lvert\lambda\rvert>1$, marginal if $\lvert\lambda\rvert=1$ and irrelevant if $\lvert\lambda\rvert<1$.

    The classification is the standard one for a map on a space of laws and it is used here in the following way; the article on the fixed points collects the ones this model has, with what is proved about each. The orbit approaches the fixed point along its stable directions and leaves it along its unstable ones; the critical model is the one whose orbit stays, and the two relevant directions of the hierarchical model are the thermal one, which measures the distance from the critical intensity, and an irrelevant or marginal partner that controls corrections. The exponents are functions of the relevant eigenvalues; the dictionary is the content of the exponent article, and the linearisation is computed in closed form only in the Gaussian case.

    The full context, including the running hypotheses and the proof, is in The renormalisation group map. To cite this assertion, link to tag-V2HD.html or to the article anchor directly.
