    A nondegenerate limit of the block averages forces a magnetised phase

    theorem · 15AV · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    Suppose that under some Gibbs measure the block averages $S_{\Lambda_n}/m^n$ converge in distribution to a random variable $Y$. If $M(\beta)=0$ then $Y=0$ almost surely. Equivalently, a nondegenerate limit of the block averages forces $M(\beta)>0$, and hence more than one Gibbs measure.

    Proof

    Assume $M(\beta)=0$. By the order-parameter theorem there is exactly one Gibbs measure $\mu$; since the extreme points of the set of Gibbs measures are those with trivial tail $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal T=\bigcap_n\mathcal F_{\Lambda_n^c}$, a unique one is trivial on $\mathcal T$ [@georgii-2011]. It is enough to show that $Y$ is $\mathcal T$-measurable and constant, and both come from the group structure.

    The limit is translation invariant. For $v\in\Lambda_k$ and $n\geq k$, translation by $v$ permutes $\Lambda_n$, so $Y_n\circ\tau_v=Y_n$ identically. Fix $v$, and pass to a subsequence along which $Y_n\to Y$ almost surely, which exists because $\lvert Y_n\rvert\leq1$ and $Y_n$ converges in distribution. For $n\geq\operatorname{lev}(v)$ the shifted variables equal the unshifted ones, so $Y\circ\tau_v=Y$ almost surely. As $v$ was arbitrary, $Y$ is invariant.

    An invariant event is a tail event. Let $A$ be invariant and $\Lambda$ finite. Choose $v$ of level above both $\Lambda$ and $n$; then $\Lambda_n+v$ is disjoint from $\Lambda$, so $\sigma_x\mapsto\sigma_{x+u+v}$ for $u\in\Lambda_n$ reads only spins outside $\Lambda$. Since $A$ is invariant, every term of the average $\frac{1}{\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert}\sum_{u\in\Lambda_n}\mathbf 1_A\circ\tau_{u+v}$ equals $\mathbf 1_A$, so $\mathbf 1_A$ is measurable with respect to the spins outside $\Lambda$. As $\Lambda$ was arbitrary, $A\in\mathcal T$.

    So $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal T$, which is trivial, and $Y$ is almost surely constant. The constant is the mean: $\lvert Y_n\rvert\leq1$, so $\mathbb E[Y]=\lim_n\mathbb E[Y_n]$, and the mean of $Y_n$ is $M_n(\beta)\to M(\beta)=0$ for the plus measures, zero at every $n$ for the free measures by the flip symmetry, and in $[-M,M]=0$ for any Gibbs measure by the block-average proposition. Hence $Y=0$ almost surely.

    The theorem is the converse that the transfer from the recursion needs, and it is where the difference between a mean and a law shows up. Above the critical intensity the free block average converges in distribution to the symmetric law on $\pm M$, whose mean is zero; the previous proposition sees nothing, since its bound $\lvert\mathbb E[S_{\Lambda_n}]\rvert\leq m^nM$ is consistent with any value of $M$ and any mean in $[-M,M]$. What rules that case out when $M=0$ is not an inequality but the structure of the tail: a unique Gibbs measure has none, so a limit of block averages cannot be a fair coin.

    The boundary field gives the same relation a quantitative form. For $t\in[-1,1]$ let $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{(t)}$ be the measure whose boundary spins are all $t$; the energy of the boundary is then $-tB_nS_{\Lambda_n}$, because the rim mass is the same at every vertex. The family is a one-parameter exponential family in $S_{\Lambda_n}$, so

    $$\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{(t)}}[S_{\Lambda_n}]=\beta B_n\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P^{(t)}}(S_{\Lambda_n}),\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{(t)}}[\sigma_x]=\beta B_n\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb P^{(t)}}(\sigma_x,S_{\Lambda_n})\geq0,$$

    the covariance being nonnegative by the FKG inequality, and integrating with the flip symmetry $\mathbb P^{(-t)}=\mathrm{flip}\,\mathbb P^{(t)}$ gives

    $$\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}}\bigl[S_{\Lambda_n}\bigr]=\tfrac12\beta B_n\int_{-1}^{1}\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P^{(t)}}(S_{\Lambda_n})\,dt.$$

    So the block magnetisation of the plus state is exactly the accumulated variance of the block spin as the boundary is turned on: $M_n(\beta)$ is the rate at which the block spin's variance is generated by the boundary, and the order parameter is its limit. This is the identity behind the statement that a nonzero block spin and a nonzero order parameter are the same phenomenon seen at two scales.

    ## Where the threshold comes from {#section-threshold}

    It is worth being explicit about why monotonicity cannot locate the transition, because the reason is structural rather than technical. Every assertion in the previous sections is preserved under replacing the kernel by another ferromagnetic one with the same total mass $C$: the domination, the sandwich, the equivalence of the theorem, and both bounds depend on the couplings only through their signs and their sum. In particular the one inequality that mentions the intensity, $M\leq\tanh(\beta C)$, holds at every $\beta$ and is therefore compatible both with $M>0$ at every intensity and with $M=0$ at every intensity. An argument whose conclusions are invariant under a change of kernel that preserves $C$ cannot produce a threshold, and the invariance here is exact, not a matter of the estimates being crude: no sharper use of the same inequalities will do better.

    The threshold is located by the recursion. In the language of the fixed-point article, the orbit of the Ising initial law under the rescaled decimation map converges to the point mass at $0$ below a critical coupling, to a nontrivial fixed point at it, and to a law of unbounded variance above it; that trichotomy is the theorem of Bleher and Sinai [@bleher-sinai-1973] [@bleher-sinai-1975]. The third branch is the magnetised phase: above the critical coupling the block spin per site has a nonzero limit $\pm M$, the two signs being the two states of this article. The identification with the order parameter defined here is exactly the statement of the block-average proposition and its converse: the block average of the plus state is the single-site magnetisation at every finite volume, it decreases to $M(\beta)$, and a nondegenerate limit of block averages is impossible when $M(\beta)=0$. So a nonzero limit of the block spin is not merely an indication of a magnetised phase — it is the same statement.

    So the division of labour is this. Monotonicity gives the shape of the phase diagram — two extremal states, an order parameter, uniqueness exactly when the order parameter vanishes — and answers the previous article's open question about how many Gibbs measures there are, in the sense that it reduces the question to the sign of one number. The recursion decides the sign. That the sign requires the recursion, and is not available from the inequalities of this article, is what the paragraph above argues, and it is the reason the wiki treats the two arguments as separate.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    No interaction. For $J=0$ the Gibbs measure is the product of the prior and is unique, so $M(\beta)=0$ for every $\beta$; the plus and minus boundary conditions have no effect beyond the boundary itself, and the two limits coincide with the product measure.

    An external field. A field $h\sum_x\sigma_x$ adds the constant $h$ to every field in the specification, and the domination proposition applies in $h$ as it does in the boundary condition: the finite-volume measures are nondecreasing in $h$, hence so is $M(\beta,h)$, and the limit $h\downarrow0$ exists and is the order parameter of this article. The field also breaks the flip symmetry, so the minus state is no longer the mirror image of the plus one, and the two magnetisations, one positive and one negative at $h=0$, move towards each other as $h$ increases. That monotonicity is what makes the phase diagram of a model with a field a pair of curves rather than a pair of symmetric ones; where they meet is not a question monotonicity answers.

    Non-example: the antiferromagnet. For $J<0$ the FKG inequality in the form used here fails, since the lattice condition has the wrong sign, and with it every step of this article: the heat bath is no longer monotone, the plus and minus boundary conditions no longer bound the Gibbs measures, and the order parameter defined above has no reason to detect anything. Recovering a monotone structure for an antiferromagnet needs a gauge transformation, that is a choice of signs $\chi$ with $\chi(x)\chi(y)=-1$ across each coupled pair, which presupposes a bipartition of the coupled pairs; on the hierarchical lattice every pair is coupled, at some level, and no such $\chi$ exists. The wiki treats ferromagnetic models throughout, and this is the hypothesis the article rests on.

    Percolation. The same architecture holds for bond percolation on the lattice: the Harris–FKG inequality gives that the connection probabilities increase in the intensity, the percolation probability $\theta(\beta)=\mathbb P_\beta(\lvert K_0\rvert=\infty)$ plays the role of the order parameter and is positive exactly in the percolating phase. The threshold there is $\beta_c$ of the percolation article, and its finiteness is decided by $\alpha<d$ through a comparison with the recursion, again not by monotonicity.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    Other boundary conditions. Every configuration $\omega$ satisfies $-\preceq\omega\preceq+$, so the sandwich holds with $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega}$ in place of $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\pm}$: the plus and minus states bound every finite-volume measure, not only the Gibbs measures. Free boundary conditions are not of that form, since there is no outside configuration to compare with, but the same domination holds: the plus measure is the free measure reweighted by $\exp\bigl(\beta\sum_{x\in\Lambda}b_x\sigma_x\bigr)$ with $b_x=\sum_{y\notin\Lambda}J(x-y)\geq0$, a bounded increasing function of the configuration, so the FKG inequality gives $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\mathrm{free}}\preceq\mathbb P_\Lambda^{+}$ and the free limit of the previous article lies between $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$ as well.

    Monotonicity in the intensity. That $M(\beta)$ is nondecreasing in $\beta$ is the first Griffiths inequality, the companion of the second used above, and is the same statement as the monotonicity of the correlations in the couplings [@griffiths-1967]. It gives a critical intensity $\beta_c=\inf\{\beta:M(\beta)>0\}$ as a well-defined number, and it is what makes the phase diagram a diagram rather than a set of values; it still does not evaluate the infimum.

    The general block system. Nothing in the argument uses the lattice beyond the fact that the blocks exhaust the vertex set and that the model is ferromagnetic with a finite total mass at a vertex. For any attractive spin system on a countable set the same two propositions hold, with the same proofs: the boundary-conditioned measures increase in the boundary configuration, the plus and minus limits exist, and every Gibbs measure lies between them. That the Gibbs measures of an attractive system form an interval in the stochastic order is the general form of the sandwich.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    The inequality that increasing functions are positively correlated under a ferromagnetic measure is due to Fortuin, Kasteleyn and Ginibre [@fkg-1971]; for Ising spins the correlation inequalities, including the pair used here, are Griffiths's [@griffiths-1967]. The extremal states of an attractive system, obtained as the monotone limits of the plus and minus boundary conditions, and the statement that every Gibbs measure lies between them, are part of the general theory in Georgii's book [@georgii-2011], where the argument is given for a general specification; the monotone coupling used above is the standard proof in the finite volume.

    The phase transition of the hierarchical Ising model, with a nonzero spontaneous magnetisation above the critical coupling, is the third branch of the trichotomy of Bleher and Sinai [@bleher-sinai-1973] [@bleher-sinai-1975]; the fixed-point article states their theorem and describes the mechanism of its proof. The separation recorded in the previous section — that the architecture is a matter of monotonicity and the threshold a matter of the recursion — is not a claim about the literature, in which the two are usually proved together for a particular model, but about which argument carries which statement.
