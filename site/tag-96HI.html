    Domination in the boundary condition and in the field

    proposition · 96HI · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    Let $\Lambda$ be finite and let $\omega\preceq\omega'$. Then $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega}\preceq\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega'}$. The same holds with an external field: if $h\leq h'$ then the measure with field $h$ is dominated by the measure with field $h'$.

    Proof by coupling the heat baths

    The heat-bath chain on $\Lambda$ updates one site at a time. Given the configuration $\sigma$ and a uniform $U$, it sets $\sigma_x=+1$ if $U\leq p_x(\sigma)$ and $-1$ otherwise, where

    $$p_x(\sigma)=\frac{1+\tanh\Bigl(\beta\sum_{y\in\Lambda\setminus x}J(x-y)\sigma_y+\beta\sum_{y\notin\Lambda}J(x-y)\omega_y+\beta h_x\Bigr)}{2}$$

    is the conditional probability that $\sigma_x=+1$ given the other spins. Each $p_x$ is increasing in $\sigma$ and in $\omega$ and in $h$, because $J>0$ and $\tanh$ is increasing. The chain is irreducible and aperiodic on the finite state space, so it has a unique invariant law, which is the Gibbs measure $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega}$, and it converges to that law from every starting point.

    Run two chains with the same uniform variables and the same choice of updated site, one for $\omega$ and one for $\omega'$, from starting configurations $\sigma\preceq\sigma'$. The domination is preserved at every update, by the monotonicity of $p_x$: if the two configurations agree at the updated site then so do the new values, and otherwise the one with the larger background has a larger flip probability, so a uniform that flips the lower chain also flips the higher one. Hence the entire path satisfies $\sigma(t)\preceq\sigma'(t)$ for all $t$. Passing to the limit in $t$ gives a coupling of the two invariant laws supported on $\{\sigma\preceq\sigma'\}$, which is domination: for increasing $f$, $\int f\,d\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega}=\mathbb E[f(\sigma)]\leq\mathbb E[f(\sigma')]=\int f\,d\mathbb P_\Lambda^{\omega'}$.

    The field case is identical, the field entering $p_x$ the same way as the boundary spin.

    Two consequences are used below. Taking $\omega\preceq\omega'$ to be the extreme configurations gives $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{-}\preceq\mathbb P_\Lambda^{+}$: the plus and minus boundary conditions are the largest and smallest available. Taking instead the boundary conditions produced by the spins outside a smaller block gives the monotonicity in the volume, which is where the limits come from.
