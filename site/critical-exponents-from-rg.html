    Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Two numbers from the fixed point, and the algebra that turns them into the exponents.

    theorem · part 2 · 8 sections · 5 tagged assertions · critical-exponents-from-rg

    A model with a renormalisation-group fixed point has two numbers that matter: the multiplier $\lambda_1$ of the linearised map at the fixed point, which says how fast a perturbation of the critical intensity is amplified per scale, and the growth exponent $\theta$ of the block spin at criticality, which says how large a block's magnetisation is at scale $L^n$. The first gives the correlation-length exponent through $\nu=\log L/\log\lambda_1$; the second gives the susceptibility exponent $\kappa=2\theta-d$, which is the same as the statement $\eta=2-\kappa$. Every other exponent follows from these two by algebra, provided the standard scaling hypotheses hold; hyperscaling is the one that can fail, and it fails in the classical regime of percolation.

    The hierarchical model contributes something that the Euclidean theory does not have: the growth exponent is not free. Because the recursion is autonomous only at $\theta=\tfrac12(d+\alpha)$, as the map article shows, any fixed point of the rescaled recursion with a finite nonzero variance has $\theta=\tfrac12(d+\alpha)$, so $\kappa=\alpha$ and $\eta=2-\alpha$ for every fixed point of this model. The nontrivial exponent is $\nu$; in the Gaussian case it takes the classical value $1/\alpha$, and at the Bleher–Sinai fixed point it does not.

    For percolation on the hierarchical lattice both statements are theorems: the two-point exponent is $2-\alpha$ for every $0<\alpha<d$ [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021], and the volume exponent satisfies $\delta\geq\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}$ above the triangle threshold, with equality conjectured. The classical long-range values are tabulated by Fisher, Ma and Nickel [@fisher-ma-nickel-1972]; the rigorous versions in the mean-field region are due to Aizenman and Fernandez [@aizenman-fernandez-1988].

    Definitions and setting#

    Let $0<\alpha<d$. Write $\tau_n(x,y)$ for the two-point function of the model on the block $\Lambda_n$ with free boundary conditions — the connection probability for percolation, the spin correlation for an Ising model — and $\tau_\beta(x,y)$ for that of the model on $\mathbb H^d_L$, when it exists. Let $\beta_c$ be the critical intensity, and for $n\geq0$ let

    $$\chi_n(\beta)=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n}\tau_n(0,y)$$

    be the block susceptibility; for percolation $\chi_n(\beta)=\mathbb E_\beta\lvert K^{\Lambda_n}_0\rvert$ is the expected size of the restricted cluster of the origin. The exponents are defined by the following asymptotic statements, each a hypothesis on the model whenever it is used below.

    $$\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n};\qquad \tau_{\beta_c}(0,y)\asymp\lVert y\rVert^{-(d-2+\eta)};\qquad \eta=2-\kappa;$$
    $$\tau_\beta(0,y)\asymp e^{-n/\xi(\beta)}\quad(\lVert y\rVert=L^n,\ \beta<\beta_c);\qquad \xi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\nu};\qquad \chi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\gamma};$$
    $$M(\beta)\asymp(\beta-\beta_c)^{\beta_{\mathrm{o}}}\ (\beta\downarrow\beta_c);\qquad M(\beta_c,h)\asymp h^{1/\delta};\qquad F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}}.$$

    Here $M$ is the spontaneous magnetisation, the limit of the mean block spin per site

    The dictionary as a calculator: choose the dimension, the scale and the kernel exponent and read the classical exponents of the Gaussian fixed point. The three scaling relations hold identically; the readout shows the residuals.
    , and $F_{\mathrm{sing}}$ the singular part of the free energy per site. The two definitions of $\eta$ agree by the calculation below: if the critical two-point function is radial and of order $\lVert y\rVert^{-(d-2+\eta)}$, the shell at level $k$ contributes $L^{dk}\cdot L^{-(d-2+\eta)k}=L^{(2-\eta)k}$ to the block susceptibility, so $\chi_n\asymp L^{(2-\eta)n}$. The content of the definition is that $\kappa$ and $\eta$ are one number read at two scales.

    Block identities#

    Two identities convert block quantities into correlations. The first is exact at finite volume and needs no assumption beyond the model.

    For the spin model of the decimation article with $S_n=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}\sigma_x$ and free boundary conditions on $\Lambda_n$,

    $$\mathbb E_\beta[S_n^2]=L^{dn}\chi_n(\beta)$$

    for every $\beta$ and every $n$.

    Proof of the block identity

    Expanding the square and taking expectations gives $\mathbb E[S_n^2]=\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_n}\tau_n(x,y)$. Translation by any $x\in\Lambda_n$ preserves $\Lambda_n$ because $\Lambda_n$ is a subgroup, and preserves the finite-volume Gibbs measure because the Hamiltonian depends only on differences and the prior is a product; hence $\tau_n(x,y)=\tau_n(0,y-x)$. Since $\Lambda_n$ is a subgroup, $y-x\in\Lambda_n$ for $x,y\in\Lambda_n$, and summing over $x$ first gives

    $$\mathbb E[S_n^2]=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}\tau_n(0,y-x)=\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert\sum_{z\in\Lambda_n}\tau_n(0,z)=L^{dn}\chi_n(\beta),$$

    the inner sum being independent of $y$ by the substitution $z=y-x$.

    The identity is the bridge between the two descriptions. The left side is what the decimation map controls directly, since the block-spin law is the state of the recursion; the right side is a correlation sum. The equality says the two growth rates are the same number: a block spin of typical size $L^{(d+\kappa)n/2}$ is exactly a block susceptibility growing like $L^{\kappa n}$.

    The second identity passes from the block sum to individual vertices, and it needs radial symmetry of the critical two-point function.

    Suppose that $\tau_{\beta_c}$ is radial, $\tau_{\beta_c}(0,y)=f(\lVert y\rVert)$, and let $g_k$ be the mean of $f$ over the shell at level $k$. If $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ then $g_k\leq CL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for every $k$, with $C$ depending only on $L,d$ and the constants in the hypothesis; and if $g_k\leq CL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for every $k$ then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\leq A'L^{\kappa n}$. Conversely, if $g_k\asymp L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$, then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$; and if in addition $k\mapsto L^{(d-\kappa)k}g_k$ is monotone, then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ implies the two-sided bound $g_k\asymp L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$.

    Proof of the shell estimates

    The shell at level $k$ has $S_k=(1-L^{-d})L^{dk}$ vertices, and $\chi_n=\sum_{k\leq n}S_kg_k$. Since all terms are nonnegative, $S_kg_k\leq\chi_k\leq AL^{\kappa k}$ for $k\leq n$, which is $g_k\leq\frac{A}{1-L^{-d}}L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$; this gives the first two statements, the second by summing the geometric series $\sum_{k\leq n}S_kCL^{-(d-\kappa)k}=C(1-L^{-d})\sum_{k\leq n}L^{\kappa k}$.

    If $g_k\geq cL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for all $k$ then the sum is at least a constant times $L^{\kappa n}$, since the last term alone is $S_ng_n\geq c(1-L^{-d})L^{\kappa n}$; this gives the converse. Under the monotonicity hypothesis write $h_k=L^{(d-\kappa)k}g_k$ and note that $\chi_n=\sum_{k\leq n}(1-L^{-d})L^{\kappa k}h_k$; if $h_k\leq h_n$ for $k\leq n$ then $\chi_n\leq(1-L^{-d})h_n\sum_{k\leq n}L^{\kappa k}\leq C'h_nL^{\kappa n}$, so $aL^{\kappa n}\leq\chi_n$ and $\chi_n\leq C'h_nL^{\kappa n}$ give $h_n\geq a/C'$, which is the lower bound; the upper bound is the first statement.

    The second proposition is the version needed for percolation, where the two-point function is known to be radial and the exponent is proved from the block sums. The averaging is essential: the block susceptibility controls the shell means, and the pointwise statement is a separate result, requiring the level-isometry symmetry of the lattice in addition.

    The growth exponent is fixed#

    Let $\nu_n$ be the law of $L^{-\theta_0n}S_n$ with $\theta_0=\tfrac12(d+\alpha)$ and suppose that $\nu_n$ converges weakly to a law $\nu^*$ with $0<\int s^2d\nu^*<\infty$, the second moments being uniformly integrable. Then

    $$\mathbb E_\beta[S_n^2]=L^{(d+\alpha)n}\int s^2d\nu^*\,(1+o(1)),\qquad \chi_n(\beta)\asymp L^{\alpha n},$$

    so $\kappa=\alpha$ and $\eta=2-\alpha$. The growth exponent is $\theta_0$ for every fixed point of the rescaled recursion, independently of the intensity and of the fixed point.

    Proof of the growth theorem

    By definition $\mathbb E[S_n^2]=L^{2\theta_0n}\int s^2d\nu_n$ and $2\theta_0=d+\alpha$. Weak convergence together with uniform integrability of the second moments gives $\int s^2d\nu_n\to\int s^2d\nu^*$, which is the first display after substituting $2\theta_0=d+\alpha$. The second follows from the block identity $\chi_n=L^{-dn}\mathbb E[S_n^2]=L^{\alpha n}\int s^2d\nu^*(1+o(1))$, and $\kappa=\alpha$ is the definition of $\kappa$.

    The mechanism is worth separating from the bookkeeping. The autonomy of the recursion at $\theta_0$ is a statement about the couplings, and it says that the only self-similar law consistent with them has block spins growing at rate $L^{\theta_0n}$. What the theorem adds is convergence of the orbit, which is the content of the fixed-point theory in the Gaussian and non-Gaussian cases. The exponent that is not fixed this way is $\nu$, which measures the rate at which the orbit leaves the fixed point and is computed from the linearisation.

    The dictionary#

    Suppose that $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ and that, uniformly in $n$ and $\beta$,

    $$\chi_n(\beta)\asymp\min\bigl\{L^{\kappa n},\ \xi(\beta)^{\kappa}\bigr\},\qquad \xi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\nu}.$$

    Then $\chi(\beta)\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\kappa\nu}$, that is $\gamma=\kappa\nu$. If in addition $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}$ then $2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=d\nu$, and if in addition $M\asymp\xi^{-(d-\kappa)/2}$ then $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$; under these two, together with the two relations just displayed,

    $$\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa},\qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d\nu,\qquad \gamma=\nu(2-\eta),\qquad \gamma=\beta_{\mathrm{o}}(\delta-1).$$
    Proof of the dictionary

    For $\gamma$: the supremum over $n$ of $\min\{L^{\kappa n},\xi^\kappa\}$ is $\xi^\kappa$ up to constants, and $\xi\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\nu}$ gives $\chi\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{-\kappa\nu}$.

    For $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}$: $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{d\nu}$, and comparing with $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{2-\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}}$ gives the identity.

    For $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}$: $M\asymp\xi^{-(d-\kappa)/2}\asymp\lvert\beta_c-\beta\rvert^{\nu(d-\kappa)/2}$, and comparing with the definition gives $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$, which is the same as $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-2+\eta)$ because $\kappa=2-\eta$.

    For $\delta$: Widom's relation $\gamma=\beta_{\mathrm{o}}(\delta-1)$ is the definition of $\delta$ once the other two exponents are known, and substituting $\gamma=\kappa\nu$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)$ gives $\kappa\nu=\tfrac{\nu}{2}(d-\kappa)(\delta-1)$, hence $\delta-1=\frac{2\kappa}{d-\kappa}$ and $\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa}$. The last two displays restate the first two relations with $\kappa=2-\eta$.

    Which of these is a theorem of the model and which is an extra hypothesis is the point of the small print. The first display of the dictionary is a theorem of percolation in the hierarchical model below the triangle threshold, where the block susceptibility is known to grow like $L^{\kappa n}$ and the truncated form is a consequence of the finite correlation length; hyperscaling, the identity $F_{\mathrm{sing}}\asymp\xi^{-d}$, is the hypothesis that fails in the classical regime. The relations $\gamma=\kappa\nu$ and the identification $\kappa=2-\eta$ are renormalisation-group statements and hold whenever the fixed point exists with a finite variance; $\delta=\frac{d+\kappa}{d-\kappa}$ follows only when hyperscaling does.

    Examples and non-examples#

    The Gaussian fixed point. With $\kappa=\alpha$ and $\nu=1/\alpha$ the dictionary gives $\gamma=1$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=\frac{d-\alpha}{2\alpha}$, $\delta=\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{hy}}=2-d/\alpha$. These are the classical long-range values and they satisfy all three scaling relations identically; see the Gaussian article, where they are computed from the recursion rather than from the dictionary.

    Hierarchical percolation. Here $\kappa=\alpha$ is a theorem for every $0<\alpha<d$ [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021], so $\eta=2-\alpha$ and $\gamma=\nu\alpha$. For $\alpha<d/3$ the model is mean-field in the sense that the triangle condition holds and $\delta=2$, $\beta_{\mathrm{o}}=1$, while $\nu=1/\alpha$; hyperscaling fails there, and indeed $\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}<2$ for $\alpha<d/3$. For $\alpha>d/3$ the volume exponent satisfies $\delta\geq\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}>2$, with equality conjectured, and the value of $\nu$ is not known. Details are in the percolation article.

    The Ising model. For $\alpha<d/2$ the exponents are the classical ones, $\nu=1/\alpha$ and $\kappa=\alpha$, by Aizenman and Fernandez [@aizenman-fernandez-1988] and, in the Euclidean case, by the lace expansion [@heydenreich-hofstad-sakai-2008]. For $\alpha>d/2$ the critical behaviour is governed by the non-Gaussian fixed point, $\nu$ differs from $1/\alpha$, and the growth exponent stays at $\kappa=\alpha$ by the theorem above. At $\alpha=d/2$ the classical values hold with logarithmic corrections.

    Non-example: a model without a finite-variance fixed point. If the rescaled orbit diverges, as it does for $\beta>\beta_c$ in the Gaussian model, no exponent is defined at that intensity: the block spin per site tends to a nonzero limit and the susceptibility is infinite. The definitions above are statements about the critical orbit and its neighbourhood only.

    Variants and generalisations#

    Finite-size scaling. The block susceptibility at criticality is the finite-size susceptibility of the block, so $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ is a finite-size scaling statement with $\kappa=\gamma/\nu$: the susceptibility of a block of side $L^n$ at criticality grows like its side to the power $\gamma/\nu$. This is the form in which the exponents are measured numerically, and it requires no infinite-volume limit.

    Corrections to scaling. The approach to a fixed point along an irrelevant direction with eigenvalue $\lambda_2$, $\lvert\lambda_2\rvert<1$, modifies each power law by a factor $1+O(L^{-n(1-\log\lvert\lambda_2\rvert/\log L)})$. A complex pair of eigenvalues produces an oscillating factor, which is what distinguishes the hierarchical model's subleading behaviour from a pure power law and is analysed at the nontrivial fixed point.

    Two independent exponents. The dictionary uses exactly two inputs, $\kappa$ and $\nu$; a model with a fixed point but no hyperscaling has two independent exponents and the other three relations fail, as in percolation for $\alpha<d/3$. Five exponents cannot be independent in a model with a one-parameter family of fixed points, which is the content of the scaling hypothesis in its general form.

    History and attribution#

    The reading of the indices off the linearised renormalisation map is the content of Bleher and Sinai's second paper on the hierarchical model [@bleher-sinai-1975], where the indices are computed as functions of the spectrum at the fixed point. The classical long-range values are those of Fisher, Ma and Nickel [@fisher-ma-nickel-1972], and the rigorous mean-field region for long-range Ising models is due to Aizenman and Fernandez [@aizenman-fernandez-1988]. The two-point exponent for hierarchical percolation and the volume-exponent obstruction are Hutchcroft's [@hutchcroft-hierarchical-2021].
