    Ferromagnetic measure, the two extremal boundary conditions, and the order parameter

    definition · 144T · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    A configuration $\omega'$ dominates $\omega$ if $\omega'_x\geq\omega_x$ for every $x$, and a function $f$ is increasing if it is increasing in each spin. For probability measures on $\Omega$, $\nu\preceq\nu'$ means $\int f\,d\nu\leq\int f\,d\nu'$ for every bounded continuous increasing $f$: stochastic domination. Define

    $$M(\beta)=\mathbb E_{\mu_+}[\sigma_0],\qquad C=\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)=\frac{J(1-L^{-d})}{L^{\alpha}-1},$$

    so that $C$ is the total coupling mass at a vertex, finite for every $\alpha>0$, and the order parameter is $M(\beta)$, the magnetisation of the plus state at the origin.

    The two facts used throughout are the FKG inequality for the ferromagnetic Ising measures, $\mathbb E[fg]\geq\mathbb E[f]\mathbb E[g]$ for bounded increasing $f$ and $g$ [@fkg-1971] [@griffiths-1967], and its consequence that the measures are monotone in their boundary condition. The second is proved here, because the proof is a coupling and the coupling is what the later arguments run on.
