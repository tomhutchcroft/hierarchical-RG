    The renormalisation group

    The map on block-spin laws, its fixed points, and the exponents read off them.

    part 2 of 3 · 9 articles · part-renormalisation-group

    The part has one object: the rescaled decimation map on laws on the line, of which the model is an orbit. It builds the operator; solves it exactly in the Gaussian case, where the orbit closes on the variance; reads off a fixed point the two numbers that matter, the multiplier of the linearisation, which gives the correlation-length exponent, and the growth of the block spin, which the autonomy of the recursion fixes for every fixed point of this model; and turns them into the exponent dictionary by algebra. That dictionary is an if-then statement, and the article after it supplies the 'if' for the hierarchical Ising model, a nontrivial fixed point with a spectrum, which is the theorem of Bleher and Sinai. With the spectrum in hand the corrections to the power laws follow from the subleading eigenvalues; the second existence proof, Koch and Wittwer's, is valid without a small parameter; and the part closes with the flow described globally rather than near a single fixed point.

    5 tagged assertions

    The renormalisation group map

    Decimation as an operator on one-variable laws, and the scaling that makes it autonomous.

    Block decimation sends the law of a child's block spin to the law of the parent's: a nonlinear operator on probability measures on the line, a Gibbs tilt of a product measure followed by summation. Rescaling the spin by L to the power minus (d+alpha)/2 makes the operators at different scales into powers of a single map, whose fixed points and linearisations carry the critical behaviour.

    6 tagged assertions

    The Gaussian renormalisation group

    On Gaussian laws the decimation closes on the variance, and the critical fixed point has multiplier L to the alpha.

    For a Gaussian prior, the law of a block spin stays Gaussian and its variance obeys a fractional linear recursion that is solved in closed form. The critical intensity is the reciprocal of the total kernel mass, the susceptibility is exactly beta_c over beta_c minus beta, and the recursion, written in the dimensionless coupling, has a nonzero fixed point with multiplier L to the alpha; hence the correlation-length exponent is one over alpha and the two-point exponent is two minus alpha.

    The spectrum at the Gaussian fixed point

    Why the quartic coupling is relevant above half the dimension, and what it is a computation of.

    At the Gaussian fixed point the linearised decimation map is triangular in the basis of even monomials, and its diagonal entries are L^(d(1-k)+k alpha) for the monomial of degree 2k. The first entry is the variance multiplier; the second is the eigenvalue of the quartic coupling, which exceeds one exactly when alpha exceeds half the dimension. The relevance of the quartic term, the crossover at alpha = d/2 and the upper critical dimension four of the nearest-neighbour model are therefore one computation.

    Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Two numbers from the fixed point, and the algebra that turns them into the exponents.

    The exponents of a hierarchical model are read off from its fixed point: one from the multiplier of the linearised map, giving the correlation-length exponent, and one from the growth of the block spin, giving the susceptibility exponent and hence the two-point exponent. The scaling relations are then algebra, and hyperscaling is the one hypothesis that is not automatic. A third route, through the concavity of the magnetisation in the boundary field, needs no fixed point: it sandwiches the finite-size magnetisation between the block-spin variances of the plus and the free states, and shows that at criticality a vanishing boundary field still pins the block spin.

    The Bleher–Sinai fixed point

    Above the threshold a non-Gaussian law is self-similar, and its multiplier is not one over alpha.

    For kernel exponent above half the dimension the critical orbit of the hierarchical recursion converges to a self-similar law that is not Gaussian: the fixed point of Bleher and Sinai and of Gawedzki and Kupiainen. Its linearisation has a simple leading eigenvalue, which gives a correlation-length exponent different from the classical one over alpha, and a spectrum whose next eigenvalue controls corrections to scaling, sometimes oscillating.

    2 tagged assertions

    Corrections to scaling

    The second eigenvalue, and why a power law is only ever the first term.

    Near a fixed point the orbit approaches along the stable directions, so every power law carries corrections from the second eigenvalue. In the hierarchical model the corrections are geometric in the scale and therefore pure powers of the correlation length, one for each subleading eigenvalue, with exponent omega equal to minus the logarithm of the subleading eigenvalue over the logarithm of the scale; a complex pair makes them oscillate in the logarithm of the length, and an eigenvalue tending to one makes them logarithmic.

    The free energy and its singularity

    The recursion summed scale by scale, the Gaussian case in closed form, and where the curvature diverges.

    The free energy of a hierarchical model is a sum over scales, one elementary term per decimation step, and for a Gaussian prior each term is a Gaussian integral in closed form. That gives the free energy of the Gaussian model as an explicit series, convergent exactly up to the critical intensity, and a curvature that diverges there. The same computation gives the classical specific-heat exponent by a scaling argument, and the argument shows why the Ising case, where no term is elementary, cannot be treated this way.

    2 tagged assertions

    Koch and Wittwer's non-Gaussian fixed point

    Convergent expansions for a fixed point with unbounded spins, large fields included.

    Koch and Wittwer proved the existence of a non-Gaussian fixed point of the hierarchical renormalisation map, in the hierarchical approximation of the three-dimensional phi-fourth fixed point, by a method that treats the large-field region: Borel summation brings the region where the perturbative expansion fails back into it, so that the theory is described by convergent expansions. The companion papers study the transformation itself, with its beta function and the relation between Dyson's and Gallavotti's hierarchies, and treat the Dyson-Baker hierarchy.

    2 tagged assertions

    Abdesselam's trajectory between two fixed points

    A whole orbit of the renormalisation group, from the Gaussian fixed point to the nontrivial one.

    A fixed point describes one scale-invariant theory; a trajectory between two fixed points describes the whole crossover from the ultraviolet to the infrared. Abdesselam constructed such a trajectory rigorously and nonperturbatively for a three-dimensional phi-fourth model with modified propagator, joining the Gaussian fixed point to an analogue of the Wilson-Fisher fixed point, by a fixed point argument in a Banach space of sequences which perturbs a nonlinear one-dimensional iteration.
