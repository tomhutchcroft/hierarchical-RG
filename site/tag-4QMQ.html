    At criticality the block-spin variance grows at most like L to the (d+alpha)n

    theorem · 4QMQ · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Let $\beta=\beta_c$ and $r=L^{d+\alpha}$, and suppose that $\mathbb E_{\beta_c}[(S_n/m^n)^2]\to0$, which holds because the block average converges to the vanishing magnetisation. Then there is $A<\infty$, depending only on $L,d,\alpha,\beta_c$, with

    $$\mathbb E_{\beta_c}\bigl[S_n^2\bigr]\ \leq\ A\,L^{(d+\alpha)n}\qquad\text{for every }n\geq0.$$
    Proof

    Put $R_n=V_n/r^n$, $B_n=V_n/m^{2n}$ and $c=\beta_c/r$, so that $\beta_cJ_nV_{n-1}=cR_{n-1}$ and (1) reads, in the two normalisations,

    $$R_n\ \geq\ Q\,h(R_{n-1})\,R_{n-1},\qquad B_n\ \geq\ h(R_{n-1})\,B_{n-1},\qquad h(R)=\frac{1+(m-1)\tanh(cR)}{m},$$

    with $Q=m^2/r=L^{d-\alpha}>1$. The function $h$ increases to $1$, so $Qh(R)$ increases to $Q>1$ and there is $A\geq1$ with $Qh(A)=:\gamma>1$. Suppose $R_N\geq A$ for some $N$. Since $h$ is increasing, the first inequality gives $R_{N+j}\geq\gamma^jA$ by induction, and the second gives $B_{N+j}\geq B_N\prod_{i<j}h(R_{N+i})$. Now $1-h(R)=\frac{m-1}{m}\bigl(1-\tanh(cR)\bigr)\leq\frac{2(m-1)}{m}e^{-2cR}$, so along a sequence growing like $\gamma^j$ the deficits are summable and the product has a strictly positive limit. Then $B_n$ does not tend to zero, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence $R_n<A$ for every $n$, which is the claim.

    So the upper half of $\kappa=\alpha$ is unconditional at criticality. The proof uses nothing about the tilt being small: the runaway argument works precisely because a large normalised variance feeds itself, so it can be excluded by the one piece of global information the model gives for free, that the block average is negligible compared with the block.

    The same argument applies at any intensity at which the block average vanishes, which is every $\beta\leq\beta_c$; above $\beta_c$ the block average is macroscopic and $R_n$ grows like $L^{(d-\alpha)n}$, consistently with the bound failing.

    ## The amplitude: a trapping argument {#section-lower}

    The upper bound says the normalised variance cannot be large. The lower bound says it cannot be small, and the proof is the mirror image of the runaway argument: where a large value is amplified, a small one decays, and decaying is as impossible as exploding because the susceptibility diverges at criticality.
