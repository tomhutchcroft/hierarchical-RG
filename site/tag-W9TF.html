    The hierarchical lattices of different dimensions are the same group

    proposition · W9TF · in The hierarchical lattice

    Let $d\geq1$ and $L\geq2$, and let $\psi:(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^d\to\mathbb Z/L^d\mathbb Z$ be a bijection taking $0$ to $0$. Applying $\psi$ in every coordinate gives a group isomorphism $\Psi:\mathbb H^d_L\to\mathbb H^1_{L^d}$ with

    $$\lVert\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)\rVert_{\mathbb H^1_{L^d}}=\lVert x-y\rVert_{\mathbb H^d_L}^{\,d}.$$
    Proof of the distance identity

    The map $\psi(a)=\sum_{j=1}^da_jL^{j-1}$ is a bijection of the underlying sets fixing $0$, and applying it coordinatewise is a bijection of the direct sums. A vertex of $\mathbb H^d_L$ has level $k$ exactly when its last nonzero entry is the $k$-th, which the coordinatewise application preserves because level is read off the index and not the entry; a vertex of $\mathbb H^1_{L^d}$ at level $k$ has distance $(L^d)^k=L^{dk}$ from the origin and distance $L^k$ in the other lattice. Group structure is not needed for the distance identity, and it holds for $\Psi$ applied to a difference since the map is applied entrywise.

    Under this identification the power kernel $\lVert x\rVert^{-d-\alpha}$ on $\mathbb H^d_L$ becomes $\lVert y\rVert^{-1-\alpha/d}$ on $\mathbb H^1_{L^d}$, so a statement about power kernels at every exponent on the one-dimensional lattice covers every dimension. The dimensional parameter is thus the ratio $\alpha/d$, which is why exponents in this model are functions of $\alpha/d$ and of the ratio of the kernel to the scale, and not of $d$ alone.

    Other coefficient groups. Nothing in the definitions uses finiteness of $A$ except the counts. With $A=\mathbb Z^d$ the level-one block is infinite, the shells are infinite, the summability criterion changes, and the model is a long-range system on a tree-like group; the child decomposition survives, the counting arguments do not.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    Dyson (1969) introduced the one-dimensional model with couplings decaying as a power and proved that a phase transition occurs for decay exponents between one and two [@dyson-1969]; the hierarchical approximation used there is the lattice above with $d=1$ and a coupling that is constant on the shells of the ultrametric. The group-theoretic description, in which blocks are cosets of nested subgroups with $L^d$ children each, is the form in which Bleher and Sinai set up the renormalisation transformation [@bleher-sinai-1973], and it is the presentation used in the modern development of the renormalisation group for lattice field theory [@bauerschmidt-brydges-slade-2019].

    The counts of this article are elementary and appear in each of those sources; the statement that all pairs between two children of a block lie at the top level of that block is the property that distinguishes this lattice from a Euclidean block partition, where the interaction between two neighbouring blocks depends on the shape of their common boundary. The comparison is the subject of Hierarchical and Euclidean renormalisation.
