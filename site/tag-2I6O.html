    The level sequence, and the two marginal values of the exponent

    definition · 2I6O · in The two marginal exponents

    The level sequence is $l=(l_k)_{k\geq1}$, and the model at exponent $\alpha$ has couplings $J_k=l_kL^{-(d+\alpha)k}$. The two marginal values are

    $$\alpha=d\ \text{(summability)},\qquad \alpha=d/2\ \text{(relevance of the quartic)}.$$

    A kernel with $\alpha=d$ and $l_k\equiv1$ is just not summable: its shells contribute a constant, so the total intensity diverges logarithmically in the level. A kernel with $\alpha=d/2$ is summable for every $d>0$, and the marginality there is a statement about the linearisation of the recursion, not about the measure.
