    Percolation, vectors, and the Euclidean comparison

    What changes when the order parameter is a set, a vector, or a field on Z^d.

    part 3 of 3 · 5 articles · part-beyond-spins

    The same block structure carries models that are not the scalar spin model, and each one changes something specific. Percolation replaces the block-spin law by a cluster partition, and the mechanism that survives is amplification: two large clusters in sibling blocks are joined at a rate proportional to the product of their sizes. Vector spins make the block spin a vector, so the direction becomes an observable, and at criticality it does not settle. The phi-fourth truncation is where the infinite-dimensionality of the flow shows up, and the Euclidean comparison is where the exactness is lost.

    3 tagged assertions

    Renormalisation for hierarchical percolation

    Cluster amplification is the relevant direction, and the triangle threshold is three alpha against d.

    For long-range percolation on the hierarchical lattice the renormalisation group acts on clusters rather than on a one-variable law, and its relevant direction is the amplification of large clusters across a block. The critical two-point function decays like the distance to the power minus d minus alpha for every exponent in the long-range range, and the triangle diagram converges exactly when three alpha is below d, which is where the model stops being mean-field.

    5 tagged assertions

    Hierarchical models with continuous symmetry

    With a vector spin the block direction does not settle at criticality, and the marginal exponent is decided by a slowly varying factor.

    For the r-component hierarchical model the decimation map acts on laws on R^r by the same tilt, and it commutes with rotations. Bleher and Major proved Dyson's conjecture for this model: at the marginal kernel exponent a transition exists exactly when the level couplings are not summable, the spontaneous magnetisation vanishes at the critical temperature, and the normalised average spin still converges to the uniform law on the sphere. The Gaussian fixed point is r-independent; the continuous symmetry enters through a marginal angular direction.

    2 tagged assertions

    The phi-fourth hierarchical model and its truncations

    The recursion is an integral operator on potentials; the Gaussian truncation is exact and every other truncation leaks.

    Written on potentials rather than on laws, the decimation step is an explicit integral operator: the effective potential of a block is minus the logarithm of a constrained convolution of the child potential. Quadratic potentials are mapped to quadratic potentials and the flow on them is the fractional linear recursion for the variance; quartic and higher truncations are not invariant, because the constrained average of a quartic generates powers of every even order. The two rigorous treatments of the resulting infinite-dimensional flow are Koch and Wittwer's fixed point and, in the Euclidean setting, the trajectory of Abdesselam.

    3 tagged assertions

    The two marginal exponents

    Alpha equal to d decides summability, alpha equal to d/2 decides relevance, and they are not the same threshold.

    Two values of the kernel exponent are distinguished in the hierarchical model and they are easy to conflate. At alpha equal to d the interaction stops being summable and the level sequence alone decides whether the model is a Gibbs system with a transition. At alpha equal to d/2 the quartic coupling is marginal at the Gaussian fixed point, so the nontrivial fixed point is relevant above that value and the classical exponents hold below it. The first threshold is about the measure, the second about the map; one is a criterion at a point, the other a bifurcation.

    Hierarchical and Euclidean renormalisation

    Why the recursion closes here, what fails on the lattice, and how the model is used anyway.

    The hierarchical recursion is exact because a block has no boundary: all pairs between two children carry one coupling, so the decimated state is a function of the child states alone. On the Euclidean lattice a block has a boundary of codimension one, the effective interaction after decimation depends on the geometry of the boundary and not on block sums, and the decimated measure need not be Gibbsian. The hierarchical model is nevertheless the reference for power counting, for the epsilon expansion and for the local analysis of the renormalisation map.
