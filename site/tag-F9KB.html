    Block-spin variance in closed form and the critical intensity

    theorem · F9KB · in The Gaussian renormalisation group

    For every $n\geq0$,

    $$v_n=\frac{m^n}{1-\beta J\,(1-L^{-d})\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}L^{-\alpha k}} ,$$

    the model on $\Lambda_n$ exists for every $n$ exactly when $\beta\leq\beta_c$, where

    $$\beta_c=\frac{1}{\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)}=\frac{1-L^{-\alpha}}{J(1-L^{-d})L^{-\alpha}},$$

    and for $\beta<\beta_c$ one has $\chi(\beta):=\lim_nv_nL^{-dn}=\dfrac{\beta_c}{\beta_c-\beta}$. At $\beta=\beta_c$ one has $v_n=L^{(d+\alpha)n}$ for every $n$, and for $\beta>\beta_c$ there is a first $n$ with $bv_n(m-1)\geq1$.

    Proof of the closed form and the critical intensity

    Put $\rho=1/m=L^{-d}$, so that $\frac{1}{v_n}=\frac{1}{mv_{n-1}}-\frac{m-1}{m}b_n$ by the recursion, and iterate from $v_0=1$:

    $$\frac{1}{v_n}=\frac{1}{m^n}\Bigl(1-(m-1)\sum_{k=1}^nm^{k-1}b_k\Bigr).$$

    Since $b_k=\beta Jm^{-(1+\alpha/d)k}$ and $m^{k-1}b_k=\beta Jm^{-1}m^{-\alpha k/d}=\beta JL^{-d}L^{-\alpha k}$, this is $\frac{1}{v_n}=L^{-dn}\bigl(1-\beta J(1-L^{-d})\sum_{k\leq n}L^{-\alpha k}\bigr)$, which is the first display after inversion. The bracket is decreasing in $n$ and its limit is $1-\beta J(1-L^{-d})\frac{L^{-\alpha}}{1-L^{-\alpha}}=1-\beta/\beta_c$ with $\beta_c$ as displayed; the row sum was computed in the lattice article. Hence the bracket stays positive for all $n$ exactly when $\beta<\beta_c$, and then $v_nL^{-dn}\to\frac{1}{1-\beta/\beta_c}=\frac{\beta_c}{\beta_c-\beta}$. At $\beta=\beta_c$ the bracket is $1-(1-L^{-\alpha n})=L^{-\alpha n}$, giving $v_n=L^{dn}L^{\alpha n}$. For $\beta>\beta_c$ the bracket is eventually negative, so the recursion leaves its domain at some finite $n$.

    The threshold has a second description, which does not use the recursion and which identifies it as the norm of the interaction rather than as an artefact of the block structure.
