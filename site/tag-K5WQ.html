    Quadratic potentials are invariant and the quartic coupling is generated

    proposition · K5WQ · in The phi-fourth hierarchical model and its truncations

    Let $V(s)=\tfrac{\mu}{2}s^2$ with $\mu>0$ and $b<1/(v(m-1))$, where $v=1/\mu$. Then $\mathcal V_bV$ is quadratic, with curvature $\mu'$ given by

    $$\frac{1}{\mu'}=\frac{m}{\mu-b(m-1)},$$

    that is $\mu'=(\mu-b(m-1))/m$: the curvature flows affinely, and the variance recursion $v'=mv/(1-bv(m-1))$ is the same statement for $v=1/\mu$. A quartic potential is not mapped to a quartic potential: at $\tfrac{\mu}{2}s^2+\lambda s^4$ with $\lambda\neq0$ the first and second orders in $\lambda$ are again quartics in $s$, but the third order is not, and a sixth power appears with a coefficient proportional to $\lambda^3$.

    Proof

    For the first part, the tilted product of Gaussians is Gaussian and the law of the sum is Gaussian, as in the closure theorem; the displayed relation is that theorem with $\mu=1/v$ and $\mu'=1/v'$.

    To locate the sixth power, condition on $S=s$ and write $s_a=s/m+\delta_a$. The constraint gives $\sum_a\delta_a=0$ identically, and the conditional law of $\delta$ is centred Gaussian on the hyperplane $\sum_a\delta_a=0$, with a covariance $\Sigma$ that does not depend on $s$. Expanding, and dropping the linear term because $\sum_a\delta_a=0$,

    $$\sum_a s_a^4=\frac{s^4}{m^3}+\frac{6s^2}{m^2}\sum_a\delta_a^2+\frac{4s}{m}\sum_a\delta_a^3+\sum_a\delta_a^4 .$$

    The cumulant expansion of $-\log\mathbb E[e^{-\lambda X}\mid S=s]$ in powers of $\lambda$ has $k$-th coefficient $\pm\frac{1}{k!}$ times the $k$-th conditional cumulant of $X=\sum_as_a^4$. The first cumulant is the conditional mean, a quartic in $s$, which is the first-order statement above. The second cumulant is the conditional variance, and since the only random coefficient of a power of $s$ in $X$ beyond the constant is $s^2$ times $\sum_a\delta_a^2$ and $s$ times $\sum_a\delta_a^3$, the variance is a quadratic form in $s^2$, $s$ and $1$: it is of degree at most four, so the second order produces no sixth power either.

    The third cumulant is a cubic form in $s^2$, $s$ and $1$, so it has a term in $s^6$: its coefficient is the third cumulant of $\frac{6}{m^2}\sum_a\delta_a^2$ under the conditional law. For a centred Gaussian vector $Z$ and a symmetric matrix $A$ one has $\kappa_3(Z^{\mathsf T}AZ)=8\operatorname{tr}\bigl((A\Sigma)^3\bigr)$, which is nonnegative, and positive here because $\Sigma$ has positive trace of its cube on the hyperplane. Hence

    $$\text{coefficient of }s^6\text{ in }-\log f_\lambda =\frac{\lambda^3}{6}\cdot\frac{6^3}{m^6}\cdot 8\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\Sigma^3\bigr)\,(1+O(\lambda)),$$

    a nonzero multiple of $\lambda^3$. The quartic family therefore fails to be preserved at third order in the quartic coupling, and the first power beyond the fourth that appears is the sixth, with a positive coefficient.

    The proposition is the precise form of two statements that are often made informally: the Gaussian truncation is exact, and every other truncation leaks. The leak is third order in the quartic coupling, so a two-coupling truncation is a controlled approximation only in a regime where the coupling is small, and the renormalisation-group flow of the hierarchical model has no such regime at the nontrivial fixed point.
