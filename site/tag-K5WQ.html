    Quadratic potentials are invariant and the quartic coupling is generated

    proposition · K5WQ · in The phi-fourth hierarchical model and its truncations

    Let $V(s)=\tfrac{\mu}{2}s^2$ with $\mu>0$ and $b<1/(v(m-1))$, where $v=1/\mu$. Then $\mathcal V_bV$ is quadratic, with curvature $\mu'$ given by

    $$\frac{1}{\mu'}=\frac{m}{\mu-b(m-1)},$$

    that is $\mu'=(\mu-b(m-1))/m$: the curvature flows affinely, and the variance recursion $v'=mv/(1-bv(m-1))$ is the same statement for $v=1/\mu$. A quartic potential is not mapped to a quartic potential: at $\tfrac{\mu}{2}s^2+\lambda s^4$ with $\lambda\neq0$ the first and second orders in $\lambda$ are again quartics in $s$, but the third order is not, and a sixth power appears with a coefficient proportional to $\lambda^3$.

    Proof

    For the first part, the tilted product of Gaussians is Gaussian and the law of the sum is Gaussian, as in the closure theorem; the displayed relation is that theorem with $\mu=1/v$ and $\mu'=1/v'$.

    To locate the sixth power, condition on $S=s$ and write $s_a=s/m+\delta_a$. The constraint gives $\sum_a\delta_a=0$ identically, and the conditional law of $\delta$ is centred Gaussian on the hyperplane $\sum_a\delta_a=0$, with a covariance $\Sigma$ that does not depend on $s$. Expanding, and dropping the linear term because $\sum_a\delta_a=0$,

    $$\sum_a s_a^4=\frac{s^4}{m^3}+\frac{6s^2}{m^2}\sum_a\delta_a^2+\frac{4s}{m}\sum_a\delta_a^3+\sum_a\delta_a^4 .$$

    The cumulant expansion of $-\log\mathbb E[e^{-\lambda X}\mid S=s]$ in powers of $\lambda$ has $k$-th coefficient $\pm\frac{1}{k!}$ times the $k$-th conditional cumulant of $X=\sum_as_a^4$. The first cumulant is the conditional mean, a quartic in $s$, which is the first-order statement above. The second cumulant is the conditional variance, and since the only random coefficient of a power of $s$ in $X$ beyond the constant is $s^2$ times $\sum_a\delta_a^2$ and $s$ times $\sum_a\delta_a^3$, the variance is a quadratic form in $s^2$, $s$ and $1$: it is of degree at most four, so the second order produces no sixth power either.

    The third cumulant is a cubic form in $s^2$, $s$ and $1$, so it has a term in $s^6$: its coefficient is the third cumulant of $\frac{6}{m^2}\sum_a\delta_a^2$ under the conditional law. For a centred Gaussian vector $Z$ and a symmetric matrix $A$ one has $\kappa_3(Z^{\mathsf T}AZ)=8\operatorname{tr}\bigl((A\Sigma)^3\bigr)$, which is nonnegative, and positive here because $\Sigma$ has positive trace of its cube on the hyperplane. Hence

    $$\text{coefficient of }s^6\text{ in }-\log f_\lambda =\frac{\lambda^3}{6}\cdot\frac{6^3}{m^6}\cdot 8\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\Sigma^3\bigr)\,(1+O(\lambda)),$$

    a nonzero multiple of $\lambda^3$. The quartic family therefore fails to be preserved at third order in the quartic coupling, and the first power beyond the fourth that appears is the sixth, with a positive coefficient.

    The proposition is the precise form of two statements that are often made informally: the Gaussian truncation is exact, and every other truncation leaks. The leak is third order in the quartic coupling, so a two-coupling truncation is a controlled approximation only in a regime where the coupling is small, and the renormalisation-group flow of the hierarchical model has no such regime at the nontrivial fixed point.

    ## Truncations and what replaces them {#section-truncations}

    A truncation is a projection of the flow onto finitely many couplings, and the proposition above shows that the projection does not commute with the flow beyond the quadratic term. Three routes replace it.

    Infinite-dimensional analysis. The flow is studied on a Banach space of potentials, with the fixed point found by an existence argument rather than by truncation. This is Koch and Wittwer's route for the scalar hierarchical model [@koch-wittwer-1986], and the setting is the one described in the article on it.

    Expansion in a parameter. Near $\alpha=d/2$ the nontrivial fixed point is close to the Gaussian one and the flow can be expanded in $\alpha-d/2$, which gives the $\varepsilon$-expansion of Gawedzki and Kupiainen [@gawedzki-kupiainen-1983] and, in the hierarchical model, of Bleher and Sinai. The expansion is a statement about a neighbourhood of the marginal exponent, not about a truncation of the potential.

    Exact solutions of special models. For a quadratic prior the flow is explicit, and for the vector model at low temperature it is explicit around a nonzero magnetisation; those are the two cases in which the recursion is solved rather than analysed.

    The $\varphi^4$ flow in two couplings nevertheless predicts the qualitative picture correctly: a Gaussian fixed point at the origin of the quartic coupling, a nontrivial fixed point at a positive quartic coupling for $\alpha>d/2$, and a critical surface separating the two phases. What it cannot do is give the position of the nontrivial fixed point or the value of $\nu$ there.

    ## The low-temperature vector model {#section-low-temperature}

    For the $r$-component model with $r\geq2$ the ordered phase has a direction, and the question a renormalisation-group analysis asks there is not about a fixed point but about the limit of the block sums around a nonzero magnetisation. Bleher and Major settle it for the vector-valued phi-fourth hierarchical model: at a supercritical intensity the centred block sums, normalised by the volume in the direction of the magnetisation and by a different power in the orthogonal directions, converge to a Gaussian field whose coordinates are independent [@bleher-major-1984].

    The two normalisations are the content. With the pair coupling at level $n$ equal to $2^{-an}$, so that $a=1+\alpha$ in the notation of this wiki, the direction of the spontaneous magnetisation scales as the square root of the volume, and each of the $r-1$ orthogonal directions scales as $\alpha_i^{n/2}$ with $\alpha_i=2^{a}=L^{1+\alpha}$. Since $\alpha_i>L$ in the long-range range, the orthogonal block sums grow faster than the square root of the volume: the transverse fluctuations are enhanced, and after that normalisation their limit is Gaussian with independent coordinates. The proof rests on the large-deviation theory of the same model [@bleher-1985].

    In the non-Gaussian case the limit is not Gaussian, and its description is the subject of the two-part paper of 1988: a limit theorem for the average spin, and a description of the large-scale limit field [@bleher-major-1988], with a conference account of the renormalisation of the low-temperature model in [@bleher-major-1990]. Read with the survey, the picture is that below the critical temperature the vector model has non-central limit theorems at every temperature, in contrast with the scalar model, where away from criticality the block sums have Gaussian limits [@bleher-major-1987].

    These are the fluctuations whose critical form is the marginal angular direction of the vector article: at $T_c$ the transverse enhancement is what makes the direction of the block spin asymptotically uniform.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    The scalar case at $\alpha>d/2$. Here the nontrivial fixed point governs, the quartic coupling is relevant, and the two-coupling truncation places the fixed point at a value of the coupling that the exact analysis corrects; the exponent $\nu$ differs from $1/\alpha$, as the fixed-point article records.

    Short-range $\varphi^4$ in three dimensions. The Euclidean model with a modified propagator, for which the renormalisation-group trajectory is constructed by Brydges, Mitter and Scoppola [@brydges-mitter-scoppola-2003], has the same structure of a Gaussian ultraviolet fixed point and a nontrivial infrared one; the hierarchical counterpart of the modified propagator is the geometric kernel of the lattice article, and the trajectory joining the two is Abdesselam's.

    Non-example: a quartic truncation at the fixed point. Setting the sixth and higher couplings to zero is not a consistent approximation at a fixed point with a nonzero quartic coupling, by the second part of the proposition; the truncated flow has a fixed point, but the potential there is not the effective potential of any model.

    Non-example: the Gaussian model with a quartic term added by hand. Adding $\lambda s^4$ to the prior without following its flow gives a model whose critical behaviour is Gaussian, because the added term is a bounded perturbation of a quadratic potential for the purposes of the recursion at small $\lambda$; it is not an approximation to the $\varphi^4$ fixed point.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    Finite-component vectors. For $r$ components the potential is a function of $\lvert s\rvert$, and the flow preserves rotation invariance; the space of radial potentials is infinite-dimensional for every $r$, and the marginal angular directions are absent from it, which is why the radial truncation is more nearly consistent than a truncation in powers of $s$.

    Hierarchical models on other geometries. The Baker-based block structure gives the same kind of potential flow with different constants, and it is the model for which Koch and Wittwer prove a nontrivial fixed point in their third paper [@koch-wittwer-1994]; the $p$-adic and ultrametric groups give further variants with the same structure [@abdesselam-2018].

    The free energy along the flow. The additive constants dropped in the definition are the per-scale increments of the free energy, so the potential flow carries the free energy as a cocycle: the increment at scale $k$ is the logarithm of the normaliser of the step. This is how the singular part of the free energy is computed from the flow in the exponent dictionary.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    The potential formulation is that of Bleher and Sinai, who wrote the recursion as a nonlinear transformation of measures and, equivalently, of the negative logarithm of their densities [@bleher-sinai-1973]. The low-temperature analysis of the vector model is Bleher and Major's [@bleher-major-1984]; the existence of the nontrivial fixed point without truncation and without a small parameter is Koch and Wittwer's [@koch-wittwer-1986]; the $\varepsilon$-expansion around the marginal exponent is Gawedzki and Kupiainen's [@gawedzki-kupiainen-1983]; and the classical long-range exponent values, with the upper critical exponent $\alpha=d/2$, are those of Fisher, Ma and Nickel [@fisher-ma-nickel-1972]. The systematic treatment of the renormalisation group as an operation on potentials is the one developed by Bauerschmidt, Brydges and Slade [@bauerschmidt-brydges-slade-2019].
