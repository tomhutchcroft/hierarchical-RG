    The order parameter is positive exactly when the Gibbs measure is not unique

    theorem · 7AD0 · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    For every $\beta\geq0$, $M(\beta)\geq0$, and $M(\beta)>0$ if and only if $\mu_+\neq\mu_-$, that is, if and only if the hierarchical Ising model has more than one Gibbs measure.

    Proof

    The global flip $\sigma\mapsto-\sigma$ maps $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{+}$ to $\mathbb P_\Lambda^{-}$ and preserves the Hamiltonian, so it maps $\mu_+$ to $\mu_-$. Hence $\mathbb E_{\mu_-}[\sigma_0]=-M(\beta)$; since $\mu_-\preceq\mu_+$ this gives $M\geq-M$, that is $M\geq0$, and $M>0$ gives $\mu_-\neq\mu_+$ at once.

    For the converse, suppose $M=0$. Both $\mu_+$ and $\mu_-$ are translation invariant: $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}$ is invariant under translation by any $v\in\Lambda_n$, because the Hamiltonian, the prior and the constant boundary condition all are, and a limit of invariant measures is invariant. So $\mathbb E_{\mu_+}[\sigma_x]=M=0$ for every $x$, and the same on the minus side.

    Couple the two states monotonically. For each $n$, the proof of the domination proposition gives a coupling of $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{-}$ and $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}$ supported on $\{\sigma\preceq\sigma'\}$. The set of probability measures on the compact space $\Omega\times\Omega$ is compact, so a subsequence of these couplings converges to a coupling $m$ of $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$ supported on $\{\sigma\preceq\sigma'\}$. Under $m$, the differences $\sigma'_x-\sigma_x$ are nonnegative with $\mathbb E_m[\sigma'_x-\sigma_x]=0-0=0$, so $\sigma'_x=\sigma_x$ $m$-a.s.; intersecting over the countably many vertices gives $\sigma=\sigma'$ $m$-a.s., and therefore $\mu_-=\mu_+$.

    The theorem is the reason the order parameter deserves the name: non-uniqueness, which is a statement about a set of measures, is detected by one number. It says nothing yet about whether that number is positive, and the next two results are everything monotonicity has to say on that question.
