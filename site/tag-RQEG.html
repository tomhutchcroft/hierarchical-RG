    The volume exponent, and the upper critical dimension it turns at

    definition · RQEG · in The volume of the critical cluster

    Write $\lvert K\rvert$ for the volume of the cluster of the origin, and define $\delta$ by

    $$\mathbb P_{\beta_c}\bigl(\lvert K\rvert\geq n\bigr)=n^{-1/\delta+o(1)},\qquad n\to\infty.$$

    The upper critical dimension of the model is $d_c=3\alpha$: the value at which the triangle diagram of the percolation article changes from convergent to divergent, and the value at which the volume exponent changes value.

    The two thresholds of the percolation model should not be confused with the two marginal exponents of the spin model. The triangle threshold $3\alpha=d$ is a property of the percolation class alone; the summability threshold $\alpha=d$ decides whether the model is a Gibbs system at all, and the quartic threshold $\alpha=d/2$ is a property of the spin model's linearisation. In one dimension they read $\alpha=1/3$, $1$ and $1/2$. The article on the marginal exponents sets the last two side by side; the first is this one.
