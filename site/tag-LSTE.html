    Every finite-volume plus state is magnetised at every positive intensity

    proposition · LSTE · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    Let $\beta>0$ and $n\geq1$. Then $M_n(\beta)>0$: the block of level $n$ with the plus boundary condition is magnetised at every vertex, however high the temperature.

    Proof

    The plus measure is the free measure on $\Lambda_n$ reweighted by the strictly increasing function $w(\sigma)=\exp\bigl(\beta\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}b_x\sigma_x\bigr)$, where $b_x=B_n=CL^{-\alpha n}>0$ is the boundary field. Its mean at a vertex is

    $$\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{+}}[\sigma_x]=\frac{\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}[\sigma_xw]}{\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}[w]}=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}[\sigma_x]+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}(\sigma_x,w)}{\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}[w]},$$

    and $\mathbb E_{\mathbb P^{\mathrm{free}}}[\sigma_x]=0$ by the flip symmetry. The covariance of the two increasing functions $\sigma_x$ and $w$ is nonnegative by the FKG inequality, and it is positive as soon as $\beta>0$, because then $w$ is not constant and the free measure is non-degenerate. Dividing by a positive normaliser gives $M_n(\beta)>0$.

    The proposition looks like it contradicts the existence of a transition, and it does not, because it is a statement about a block. On a finite block the boundary is at a finite distance from every vertex, so the strictly positive boundary field magnetises it at any intensity; the transition is the statement that the limit of $M_n$ can vanish, which is compatible with every term being positive and is why the sign of $M$ is a question about the limit and not about any volume. The same distinction is what makes the block-average proposition say that the block average decreases to $M$: it decreases, and the question is whether its limit is zero.
