    Gaussian closure and the variance recursion

    theorem · N1GK · in The Gaussian renormalisation group

    Let $b\geq0$ and let $\mu=N(0,v)$ with $v>0$. If $bv(m-1)<1$ then $Z_b(\mu)<\infty$ and

    $$\mathcal T_b\mu=N(0,v'),\qquad v'=\frac{mv}{1-bv(m-1)};$$

    if $bv(m-1)\geq1$ then $Z_b(\mu)=\infty$ and $\mathcal T_b\mu$ is undefined.

    Proof of the closure and the variance recursion

    Write $s=(s_a)_{a\in A}$ and let $\Sigma^{-1}=(v^{-1}+b)I-b\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$, where $\mathbf 1$ is the vector with all entries $1$. Completing the square,

    $$-\frac{1}{2v}\sum_as_a^2+\frac b2\Bigl(\bigl(\sum_as_a\bigr)^2-\sum_as_a^2\Bigr)=-\tfrac12s^T\Sigma^{-1}s,$$

    so the tilted measure is $N(0,\Sigma)$ when $\Sigma^{-1}$ is positive definite, and in that case the law of $\sum_as_a$ is $N(0,\mathbf 1^T\Sigma\mathbf 1)$. Positive definiteness of $\Sigma^{-1}$ means $v^{-1}+b>0$ and the determinant is positive; the eigenvalues of $\Sigma^{-1}$ are $v^{-1}+b$ with multiplicity $m-1$ and $v^{-1}+b-bm$ with multiplicity one, so the condition is $v^{-1}>b(m-1)$, which is $bv(m-1)<1$.

    For the variance, apply the Woodbury identity $(A-uu^T)^{-1}=A^{-1}+\frac{A^{-1}uu^TA^{-1}}{1-u^TA^{-1}u}$ with $A=(v^{-1}+b)I$ and $u=\sqrt b\,\mathbf 1$. Then $\mathbf 1^TA^{-1}\mathbf 1=\frac{mv}{1+bv}$, so

    $$\mathbf 1^T\Sigma\mathbf 1=\frac{mv}{1+bv}+\frac{b\,(mv/(1+bv))^2}{1-bmv/(1+bv)}=\frac{mv}{1+bv-bmv}=\frac{mv}{1-bv(m-1)}.$$

    If $bv(m-1)\geq1$, the quadratic form $s^T\Sigma^{-1}s$ takes nonpositive values on the line $\mathbb R\mathbf 1$, so the tilted density is not integrable and the normaliser diverges.

    The recursion is fractional linear in $v$, increasing and convex on its domain, and it blows up at the boundary $v=1/(b(m-1))$. It is the exact statement that the block spin of a Gaussian model is Gaussian: the tilt by a function of the sum and the pushforward by the sum preserve the class of centred Gaussians.
