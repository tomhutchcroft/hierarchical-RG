    Bauerschmidt, Brydges and Slade: the method, and the four-dimensional case

    reading map · source-bauerschmidt-brydges-slade

    This is the reading map for the one book in the catalogue that is not a paper: the Lecture Notes volume in which Roland Bauerschmidt, David Brydges and Gordon Slade develop their renormalisation group method, in the setting of the four-dimensional n-component |φ|⁴ model. The group is one work, so the map is not a survey of a literature; it says what the book is for, which parts of this wiki lean on it, and what it does not cover.

    The method is the one that made the four-dimensional case rigorous, and its organisation is worth knowing before opening the book. The renormalisation group is presented as a flow of potentials rather than of measures: one writes the effective potential after a decimation step as a sum of local polynomials, and the whole analysis is the control of the remainder under that flow. Two devices carry the control: a family of seminorms in which the flow is a contraction on the irrelevant directions, and a perturbation expansion whose terms are indexed by the scale at which they first appear. The book develops these for the hierarchical model first, because there the decimation step is an identity and the flow is a recursion on one variable; the Euclidean model is the target and the reason the machinery has to be as heavy as it is.

    What this wiki takes from it

    Three things, and none of them is a theorem about the hierarchical model of this wiki.

    The potential formulation. This wiki writes the recursion on the law of the block spin, which is Bleher and Sinai's form. The book writes it on the negative logarithm of the density, which is the form in which the flow of couplings appears as a differential equation and in which truncations can be controlled. The two are the same recursion, and the phi-fourth article says where they part company: the law carries the whole measure, the potential only its logarithm, and a truncation of the potential is not a truncation of the law.

    Power counting as the linearisation of a map at a Gaussian fixed point. The eigenvalues L^{d(1-k)+kα} of the spectrum article are usually introduced as dimensional analysis. The book introduces them as the derivative of a map, which is what they are, and that is the presentation this wiki follows.

    The reason the Euclidean case needs more. The hierarchical model is not a warm-up in the book; it is the case in which the method closes, and the Euclidean case is where the seminorms, the localisation theorem and the supersymmetric representation earn their place. For this wiki the value is the contrast: it shows what an argument needs when the blocks have boundaries.

    What it does not give this wiki

    The book is about the marginal dimension. Its subject is the four-dimensional |φ|⁴ model, where the quartic coupling is marginal and the flow has to be controlled at the marginal direction; the long-range models of this wiki have an exponent α that plays the role of the distance from the marginal dimension. The correspondence is made in the phi-fourth article and it is not an identity: changing α changes the kernel, whereas changing the dimension changes the lattice.

    Nothing in the book decides the questions this wiki leaves open about the hierarchical model itself: the upper bound on the free block-spin variance that would certify κ = α (the variance route), or the existence of a magnetised phase without the recursion. Those are questions about a model the book uses as a setting, not about the method it develops.
