    Summability of power kernels and the exact row sum

    proposition · Q6HD · in The hierarchical lattice

    For real $\alpha$ and $J\geq0$,

    $$\sum_{x\neq0}\lVert x\rVert^{-d-\alpha}<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha>0,$$

    and for $\alpha>0$ the row sum of the kernel $J\lVert x\rVert^{-d-\alpha}$ is

    $$\sum_{x\neq0}J\lVert x\rVert^{-d-\alpha}=(1-L^{-d})\,J\,\frac{L^{-\alpha}}{1-L^{-\alpha}}.$$
    Proof of the summability criterion

    The shell at level $k$ has $(1-L^{-d})L^{dk}=L^{dk}-L^{d(k-1)}$ vertices, all at distance $L^k$, so the sum is $(1-L^{-d})\sum_{k\geq1}L^{dk}L^{-(d+\alpha)k}=(1-L^{-d})\sum_{k\geq1}L^{-\alpha k}$. All terms are nonnegative, so the sum may be regrouped. The geometric series $\sum_{k\geq1}L^{-\alpha k}$ converges exactly when $L^{-\alpha}<1$, that is when $\alpha>0$, and then equals $L^{-\alpha}/(1-L^{-\alpha})$.

    Since $\alpha>0$, the kernel is integrable and the intensity of the interaction at a vertex is finite; this is the sense in which the model is nearest-neighbour-like despite its long range. At $\alpha=0$ each shell contributes the same mass $1-L^{-d}$ and the sum diverges; for $\alpha<0$ the mass of the shells grows.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    The level-2 block. Take $d=1$, $L=2$, so that $m=2$ and $\Lambda_2$ has four vertices. With $e_2(1)$ the sequence $(0,1,0,\ldots)$ and $e_1(1)=(1,0,\ldots)$, the children of $\Lambda_2$ are $\{0,e_1(1)\}$ and $\{e_2(1),e_1(1)+e_2(1)\}$, each a translate of $\Lambda_1$; the four pairs between the children have distance $4$, and the two pairs inside them have distance $2$. A coupling that charges pairs by distance therefore charges the within-child pairs at $J_1$ and the between-child pairs at $J_2$, so the block splits into two interacting copies of itself.

    The geometric kernel. The kernel $J(x)=Cr^{\operatorname{lev}(x)}$ on $\mathbb H^d_L$, with $C\geq0$ and $0\leq r<L^{-d}$, is summable, with row sum $C(1-L^{-d})r/(1-L^dr)$. This family is closed under rescalings of the level, and it contains the power kernel at $r=L^{-(d+\alpha)}$; a Gaussian model with such a kernel is exactly solvable, which is the subject of the Gaussian article.

    A pair-dependent kernel. If $J(x,y)$ depends on the pair and not on the level, as for instance a kernel that charges only pairs inside a fixed family of blocks, then the children of a block are still congruent but the energy of the block is no longer a function of the child sums. The decimation identity of the next article is specific to kernels that are constant on levels.

    The borderline exponent. At $\alpha=0$ the power kernel is not summable and the corresponding percolation model is $\beta_c=0$ for every $\beta>0$: every vertex has infinitely many neighbours of infinite total intensity. The condition $\alpha>0$ is therefore not a technical convenience in the hypotheses below.

    The full product. The compact group $\prod_{i\geq1}A$, with the same definition of level for pairs differing in finitely many coordinates, is not $\mathbb H^d_L$: it is uncountable, and the distance between two of its points can be zero even when the points differ, at coordinates tending to infinity. All statements here concern the direct sum.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    Dimension as bookkeeping. Only $m=L^d$ and the ratios $J_k/J_{k+1}=L^{d+\alpha}$ enter the definitions, so a model is parametrised by the integer $m\geq2$, the ratio $\lambda>m$ and the amplitude $J$, and the pair $(d,\alpha)$ records a choice of scale: $d=\log m/\log L$ and $\alpha=\log(\lambda/m)/\log L$. Fixing integer $d$ costs nothing.
