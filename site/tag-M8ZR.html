    Ultrametricity, nested blocks and exact shell counts

    proposition · M8ZR · in The hierarchical lattice

    The level is subadditive under addition, $\operatorname{lev}(u+v)\leq\max\{\operatorname{lev}(u),\operatorname{lev}(v)\}$, and invariant under negation. Consequently $\lVert x-z\rVert\leq\max\{\lVert x-y\rVert,\lVert y-z\rVert\}$ for all $x,y,z$, the distance is symmetric and translation invariant, and $\lVert x\rVert=0$ only at $x=0$. Each $\Lambda_n$ is a subgroup, the blocks of a fixed level are its cosets, and two blocks are disjoint or one contains the other. Finally

    $$\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert=L^{dn}=m^n,\qquad \bigl\lvert\{x:\operatorname{lev}(x)=n\}\bigr\rvert=L^{dn}-L^{d(n-1)}\quad(n\geq1).$$
    Proof of ultrametricity and the counts

    Let $u,v\in\mathbb H^d_L$ and $k=\max\{\operatorname{lev}(u),\operatorname{lev}(v)\}$. Both vanish above coordinate $k$, hence so does $u+v$, and $\operatorname{lev}(u+v)\leq k$; the coordinates of $-u$ are the negatives of those of $u$, so $\operatorname{lev}(-u)=\operatorname{lev}(u)$. Taking $u=x-y$ and $v=y-z$ gives the strong triangle inequality. Symmetry is $\operatorname{lev}(y-x)=\operatorname{lev}(x-y)$, translation invariance is $\operatorname{lev}((x+w)-(y+w))=\operatorname{lev}(x-y)$, and positive definiteness is the definition together with $\operatorname{lev}(x)\geq1$ for $x\neq0$.

    The condition $\operatorname{lev}(z)\leq n$ is exactly $z_i=0$ for $i>n$, and the set of such $z$ is closed under addition and negation, so $\Lambda_n$ is a subgroup; it is contained in $\Lambda_{n+1}$. A translate of a subgroup is a coset, and two cosets of the same subgroup coincide or are disjoint; nested subgroups give the same statement for blocks of different levels.

    Restriction to the first $n$ coordinates maps $\Lambda_n$ bijectively onto $A^n$, with inverse given by appending zeros, so $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert=\lvert A\rvert^n=m^n=L^{dn}$. The shell at level $n$ is $\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_{n-1}$ and the two blocks are nested, so its cardinality is the difference.

    Every vertex of $\Lambda_n$ is within distance $L^n$ of every other: a pair of points of $\Lambda_n$ differs only in coordinates at most $n$, so its level is at most $n$. This is the sense in which a block has no boundary: the diameter of $\Lambda_n$ is exactly the radius at which it first appears.

    ## The child decomposition {#section-children}

    The single structural fact behind the exactness of decimation is that a block is the disjoint union of congruent children, and that the interaction between two children is a function of their total spins alone. For $a\in A$ and $n\geq1$ let $e_n(a)$ be the vertex whose $n$-th coordinate is $a$ and whose other coordinates vanish.
