    Hierarchical spin systems and decimation

    The block spin of a hierarchical model is distributed as a functional of independent copies of the block spin one scale down.

    theorem · Hierarchical models · 9 sections · 5 tagged assertions · hierarchical-gibbs-measure

    A hierarchical spin system is a spin model on $\mathbb H^d_L$ whose coupling between two vertices is a function of their level alone, so that the coupling at level $k$ has one value $J_k=JL^{-(d+\alpha)k}$. Its energy on a block splits exactly into the energies of the children of that block plus a single interaction between the child sums: pairs inside a child are at levels below the top, pairs in different children are all at the top level, and no pair inside the block is at a level above it. The partition function therefore factorises, and the law of a block spin is the image of the law one scale down under an explicit operation — block decimation — which acts on the single variable $\sum_{x\in\text{child}}\sigma_x$.

    This article states that identity, its iterates, and the free energy recursion that follows; the operation itself is studied in the next article and solved exactly in the Gaussian case. The identity is what is special about the lattice: on $\mathbb Z^d$ a decimation step produces an interaction that depends on the shape of the boundary between blocks, whereas here it produces one number per block.

    The structure is Dyson's (1969), who introduced the hierarchical approximation to prove a phase transition for power-law couplings [@dyson-1969], and it is the setting of the renormalisation analysis of Bleher and Sinai [@bleher-sinai-1973]. Only free boundary conditions are used, so every object below is finite-dimensional and every identity is exact at finite volume.

    Definitions and setting#

    Fix integers $L\geq2$, $d\geq1$ and a real $\alpha>0$, write $m=L^d$, and let $J>0$. Let $\varrho$ be a probability measure on $\mathbb R$ with $\int\sigma^2\varrho(d\sigma)<\infty$; the two cases used below are the Ising prior $\varrho=\tfrac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)$ and the Gaussian prior $\varrho=N(0,1)$. Spins are real numbers $\sigma_x$.

    For finite $S\subset\mathbb H^d_L$ and $\beta\geq0$, the hierarchical Hamiltonian is

    $$H_S(\sigma)=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset S}J(\lVert x-y\rVert)\sigma_x\sigma_y,\qquad J(L^k)=J_k=JL^{-(d+\alpha)k},$$

    the partition function and Gibbs measure are

    $$Z_S(\beta)=\int_{\mathbb R^S}\exp\bigl(-\beta H_S(\sigma)\bigr)\prod_{x\in S}\varrho(d\sigma_x),\qquad \mathbb P_S(d\sigma)=\frac{e^{-\beta H_S(\sigma)}}{Z_S(\beta)}\prod_{x\in S}\varrho(d\sigma_x),$$

    and the block spin of $S$ is $S_S=\sum_{x\in S}\sigma_x$. For $n\geq0$ put $Z_n=Z_{\Lambda_n}$, $\mathbb P_n=\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}$ and $\mu_n$ for the law of $S_{\Lambda_n}$ under $\mathbb P_n$, so that $\mu_0=\varrho$. The two-point function is $\tau_\beta(x,y)=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}}[\sigma_x\sigma_y]$ for any $n$ with $x,y\in\Lambda_n$, a definition independent of $n$ because the Hamiltonian is a sum over pairs.

    Pairs at level $k$ are those in different children of a common level-$k$ block, by the child decomposition, so the Hamiltonian may be regrouped by level. That regrouping is the first statement.

    The level decomposition#

    For $n\geq1$ let $\Lambda^{(a)}=e_n(a)+\Lambda_{n-1}$, $a\in A$, be the children of $\Lambda_n$ and let $Q_k(\sigma)$ be the sum of $\sigma_x\sigma_y$ over the pairs at level $k$. Then

    $$-\beta H_{\Lambda_n}(\sigma)=\sum_{a\in A}\bigl(-\beta H_{\Lambda^{(a)}}(\sigma)\bigr)+\beta J_n\sum_{a

    and $-\beta H_{\Lambda_n}=\beta\sum_{k=1}^nJ_kQ_k$.

    Proof of the level decomposition

    Every pair $\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_n$ lies in a unique pair of children, since the children partition $\Lambda_n$. If $x,y$ lie in the same child, the child is a coset $e_n(a)+\Lambda_{n-1}$ and levels are translation invariant, so the pair's level is at most $n-1$; the term it contributes is one of the pairs summed by $H_{\Lambda^{(a)}}$, and conversely every pair of a child is a pair of $\Lambda_n$ with the same level. If $x,y$ lie in different children, the pair has level exactly $n$ by the child decomposition, so it contributes $J_n\sigma_x\sigma_y$, and summing over the pairs of the two children gives $J_nS_aS_{a'}$. No pair of $\Lambda_n$ lies at a level above $n$, because every vertex of $\Lambda_n$ vanishes above coordinate $n$ and a difference of two of them therefore does. Collecting the terms of level $k$ gives the second display.

    The regrouping shows where the exactness comes from: the interaction between two children is a function of their two sums, and the interaction between a child and all the others is a function of its sum as well, so a child's influence on the rest of the block is carried by one number.

    The decimation identity#

    For $n\geq1$,

    $$Z_n(\beta)=Z_{n-1}(\beta)^m\int_{\mathbb R^m}\exp\Bigl(\beta J_n\sum_{a

    and the law of $(S_a)_{a\in A}$ under $\mathbb P_n$ is the normalised version of that measure. Hence, writing

    $$\mathcal T_b\mu(A')=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R^m}\mathbf 1_{A'}\Bigl(\sum_{a}s_a\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(b\sum_{a

    for $b\geq0$, the block-spin laws satisfy $\mu_n=\mathcal T_{\beta J_n}\mu_{n-1}$.

    Proof of the decimation identity

    Write $\sigma=(\sigma^{(a)})_{a\in A}$ with $\sigma^{(a)}$ the restriction to the child $\Lambda^{(a)}$, and $S_a=\sum_{x\in\Lambda^{(a)}}\sigma^{(a)}_x$. The prior is a product over vertices, hence over children, and the level decomposition gives

    $$\exp(-\beta H_{\Lambda_n}(\sigma))=\prod_{a\in A}\exp\bigl(-\beta H_{\Lambda^{(a)}}(\sigma^{(a)})\bigr)\exp\Bigl(\beta J_n\sum_{a

    Because a child is a translate of $\Lambda_{n-1}$ and the Hamiltonian only depends on differences, the integral of the $a$-th factor against the prior over that child is $Z_{n-1}$ on the set where $S_a=s_a$, as the definition of the Hamiltonian gives the same function of the translated configuration. Integrating out all children with $S_a$ held fixed therefore produces $\prod_aZ_{n-1}\mu_{n-1}(ds_a)$, and integrating the remaining factor over $(s_a)$ gives the displayed identity for $Z_n$. Dividing by $Z_n$ gives the law of $(S_a)$, and the law of $\sum_as_a$ is its image.

    In this form the identity is the renormalisation transformation of Bleher and Sinai, who made the law of the block spin the object of study [@bleher-sinai-1973].

    The display is the whole renormalisation group, at finite volume: the state of the model after $n$ scales is the law of one number, and one more scale acts on that law by a fixed rule depending on the coupling $b=\beta J_n$. Three features are immediate. First, $\mathcal T_b$ is a nonlinear but explicit operation, given by a Gibbs tilt of a product measure by the variance-type quantity $\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'}=\tfrac12\bigl((\sum_as_a)^2-\sum_as_a^2\bigr)$; second, the coupling argument decreases geometrically, $J_n=L^{-(d+\alpha)n}J$; third, the identity holds for any prior $\varrho$, so it applies to Ising, Gaussian and $\varphi^4$ spins at once, differing only in the initial law $\mu_0$.

    Iteration and the free energy#

    For $1\leq k\leq n$, $\mu_n=\mathcal T_{\beta J_n}\cdots\mathcal T_{\beta J_{k+1}}\mu_k$. With $F_n=m^{-n}\log Z_n$,

    $$F_n=F_0+\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{1}{m^k}\log\mathbb E_{\mu_{k-1}^{\otimes m}}\Bigl[\exp\Bigl(\beta J_k\sum_{a

    If the prior satisfies $\lvert\sigma\rvert\leq1$ and $J_k>0$ for every $k$, then $F_n$ is nondecreasing in $n$ and

    $$F_0\leq F_n\leq F_0+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\sum_{x\neq0}J\lVert x\rVert^{-d-\alpha}$$

    for every $n$, so $F=\lim_nF_n$ exists and is finite; $F$ is convex in $\beta$.

    Proof of the free energy recursion

    The iterate is the decimation identity applied $n-k$ times. Taking logarithms in the identity for $Z_n$ and dividing by $m^n=m\cdot m^{n-1}$ gives $F_n=F_{n-1}+m^{-n}\Lambda_n$ with $\Lambda_n$ the logarithm in the display, and iterating this relation gives the stated formula.

    For the monotonicity, apply the second Griffiths inequality [@griffiths-1967] to the ferromagnetic measure $\mathbb P_{k-1}^{\otimes m}$, whose couplings between distinct children are all zero: it gives $\mathbb E[s_as_{a'}]\geq0$ for $a\neq a'$. Hence $\mathbb E[\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'}]\geq0$, and Jensen's inequality $\log\mathbb E[e^X]\geq\mathbb E[X]$ gives $\Lambda_k\geq0$, so $F_n-F_{n-1}\geq0$.

    For the upper bound, $\lvert\sigma_x\sigma_y\rvert\leq1$ gives $\exp(-\beta H_{\Lambda_n})\leq\exp(\beta\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_n}J(\lVert x-y\rVert))$, so $Z_n\leq(\int\varrho)^{m^n}\exp(\beta\sum_{\{x,y\}}J(\lVert x-y\rVert))$ and $F_n\leq F_0+\beta m^{-n}\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda_n}J(\lVert x-y\rVert)$. Each vertex of $\Lambda_n$ has at most $\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)$ total intensity to its partners, so the double sum is at most $m^n\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)/2$, which is finite by the summability criterion. Convexity follows because $\beta\mapsto\log Z_n(\beta)$ has second derivative the variance of $H_{\Lambda_n}$.

    For the Ising prior, $F_0=\log2$, and the bounds read $\log2\leq F_n\leq\log2+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{x\neq0}J(\lVert x\rVert)$. The recursion is the useful statement: the increment at scale $k$ is a function of the level-$(k-1)$ block-spin law alone, so the free energy is a sum of per-scale contributions, each computed from the renormalised state at that scale. This is the form in which the free energy is used in the article on exponents.

    Correlations of block sums#

    The derivative of the free energy in a level coupling identifies a block correlation, and this is the observable the renormalisation group controls.

    For $k\geq1$ let $T_k=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_k}[S_1S_2]$, where $S_1$ and $S_2$ are the sums over two distinct children of $\Lambda_k$; by the symmetry of the model on $\Lambda_k$ this does not depend on which two children are chosen. Then

    $$\frac{\partial}{\partial J_k}\log Z_k(\beta)=\beta\,\frac{m(m-1)}{2}\,T_k,\qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial J_k}\log Z_n(\beta)=\beta\,\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}[Q_k]\quad(n\geq k).$$
    Proof of the derivative identity

    Differentiating $Z_n=\int\exp(\beta\sum_{j\leq n}J_jQ_j)\prod_{x\in\Lambda_n}\varrho(d\sigma_x)$ in $J_k$ brings down the factor $\beta Q_k$, so the derivative is $\beta\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}[Q_k]$, which is the second identity.

    For $n=k$ the only level-$k$ block in $\Lambda_k$ is $\Lambda_k$ itself, so $Q_k$ is the sum of $S_aS_{a'}$ over the unordered pairs of distinct children, $m(m-1)/2$ terms. An isometry of $\mathbb H^d_L$ that fixes $0$ and preserves $\Lambda_k$ permutes its children, and it preserves the Hamiltonian because the Hamiltonian depends on distances, hence preserves $\mathbb P_k$; so every term has the same expectation $T_k$, and the first identity follows.

    Two cautions attach to this identity. The derivative is taken in a coupling that is present in the model, so it computes the block-sum correlation at the same intensity and not at a shifted one; and the second identity is a statement about $\mathbb P_n$, in which the spins of a level-$k$ block also interact with the rest of $\Lambda_n$, so it does not reduce to $T_k$ times the number of blocks. What the identity controls is the correlation of child sums, not of individual spins. For the Gaussian prior the two are related by a linear factor, which is how the exact solution obtains its two-point function; for other priors individual-spin correlations require separate arguments.

    Examples and non-examples#

    Ising spins. With $\varrho=\tfrac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)$, each $s_a$ is an integer between $-m^{n-1}$ and $m^{n-1}$, and $\mu_0$ is uniform on $\{\pm1\}$. The decimation identity reproduces the standard block-spin recursion of the Dyson model: the level-$n$ measure is $m$ independent copies of the level-$(n-1)$ measure, reweighted by a ferromagnetic coupling between their sums.

    Gaussian spins. With $\varrho=N(0,1)$ the same identity holds, and both the tilt and the pushforward preserve Gaussianity, so the recursion closes on the variance. This is the Gaussian renormalisation group, and it is the only case in which the recursion is solved in closed form.

    A kernel that is not constant on levels. If $J$ depends on the pair rather than on the level, the first display fails: the interaction between two children is then a sum of terms with different intensities, and the block's energy depends on the internal arrangement of spins, not only on the sums. The Euclidean obstruction is of this kind but worse, since there the geometry of the blocks also changes with scale.

    Periodic boundary conditions. With the torus $\mathbb Z^d$ replacing $\mathbb H^d_L$ there is no ultrametric and no analogue of the level decomposition. Within the hierarchical lattice one can impose a "periodic" condition by identifying $\Lambda_n$ with a quotient of $\mathbb H^d_L$, but the resulting model is a finite graph whose blocks at the top have no children above them, and the identity then describes one decimation step only.

    Variants and generalisations#

    External field. Adding $-h\sum_x\sigma_x$ to the Hamiltonian multiplies the exponent of the decimation identity by $e^{h\sum_as_a}$, so the recursion becomes one for the law of the block spin with a field argument: $\mu_n=\mathcal T_{\beta J_n,h}\mu_{n-1}$. The field is relevant at the level of single spins and must be rescaled together with the spin.

    Other priors. A $\varphi^4$ prior $e^{-V(\sigma)}d\sigma$ with $V$ a quartic polynomial gives the same identity with $\mu_0$ not Gaussian, and the recursion no longer closes: it generates an infinite-dimensional family of laws. Controlling that family is the content of the Bleher–Sinai analysis.

    Infinite volume. The measures $\mathbb P_n$ are not consistent under restriction, because a restricted Gibbs measure is not Gibbsian for the restricted Hamiltonian; the infinite-volume limit requires a boundary condition and a compactness argument. Every identity above is finite-volume and exact, which is what the renormalisation group needs; passing to the limit is a separate step, taken in the articles on the fixed point and on exponents.

    History and attribution#

    Dyson (1969) introduced the block structure and the coupling $J_k$ decaying as a power to exhibit a phase transition in one dimension [@dyson-1969]. Bleher and Sinai (1973) made the block-spin law the object of study and wrote the recursion of this article as a nonlinear transformation of probability measures [@bleher-sinai-1973]; the presentation in terms of a level decomposition of the Hamiltonian is the one used in the modern renormalisation-group treatment of lattice field theory [@bauerschmidt-brydges-slade-2019]. The positivity of the block-sum correlations used for the free energy bound is a case of Griffiths's correlation inequalities for ferromagnetic systems [@griffiths-1967].
