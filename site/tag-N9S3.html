    The plus and minus limits exist and sandwich every Gibbs measure

    proposition · N9S3 · in The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives

    The sequence $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}$ is decreasing in $n$ in the stochastic order and $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{-}$ is increasing, so both converge weakly; write $\mu_+$ and $\mu_-$ for the limits. They satisfy $\mu_-\preceq\mu_+$, and every Gibbs measure $\nu$ satisfies $\mu_-\preceq\nu\preceq\mu_+$. Consequently $\mu_+=\mu_-$ if and only if the model has exactly one Gibbs measure, and then it is that measure.

    Proof

    The sequences are monotone. Let $f$ be bounded, continuous and increasing, and local on $\Lambda_n$. Under $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n+1}}^{+}$, the conditional law of the spins in $\Lambda_n$ given the rest is $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega}$, where $\omega$ is the configuration $\sigma$ itself, which is $+1$ outside $\Lambda_{n+1}$ and takes the values $\pm1$ in $\Lambda_{n+1}\setminus\Lambda_n$. Every configuration satisfies $\omega\preceq+$, so by the domination proposition $\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega}}[f]\leq\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}}[f]$, and the tower property gives

    $$\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n+1}}^{+}}[f]=\mathbb E\Bigl[\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega}}[f]\Bigr]\leq\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}}[f].$$

    So $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n+1}}^{+}\preceq\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}$: the plus measure decreases as the block grows, which is what one expects, since a smaller block has the positive boundary closer. The same computation with $\omega\succeq-$ gives that $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{-}$ increases. A monotone sequence of probability measures on a compact space converges, so both limits exist. Since $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{-}\preceq\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{+}$ for every $n$, the limits satisfy $\mu_-\preceq\mu_+$.

    The sandwich. Let $\nu$ be a Gibbs measure, let $f$ be bounded, continuous and increasing on $\Lambda_n$, and let $\Lambda_m\supset\Lambda_n$. By the Gibbs property, $\nu$ is the average of the conditional laws $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_m}^{\omega}$ against the law of $\omega$ under $\nu$; every $\omega$ satisfies $\omega\preceq+$, so $\mathbb E_\nu[f]\leq\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_m}^{+}}[f]$, and letting $m\to\infty$ gives $\mathbb E_\nu[f]\leq\mathbb E_{\mu_+}[f]$. The other side is the same with $-$.

    Consequences. If $\mu_+=\mu_-$ then every Gibbs measure equals the common value, so it is unique. If the Gibbs measure is unique then $\mu_+$ and $\mu_-$ are both Gibbs measures and hence equal. Both states are Gibbs measures by the existence theorem, applied to the fixed boundary conditions.

    The proposition also settles the question left open by the previous article, in the ferromagnetic case: the set of Gibbs measures is nonempty, and it is an interval in the stochastic order with endpoints that are explicit. That is the whole architecture of the phase diagram, and it was obtained without computing anything.
