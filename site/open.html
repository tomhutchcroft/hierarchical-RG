    What is not known

    8 open questions

    A wiki that attributes its theorems should be as explicit about what it does not prove. This page collects the places where the argument stops short of the statement, with the reason and the article where the gap sits. Every item is either a question the wiki leaves open or a claim it carries only on someone else's authority, and the distinction is made in the text of the articles themselves, with the tags and the small print.

    1. Is the two-point function bounded pointwise? — The growth exponent and its amplitude are settled: the normalised second moment is bounded away from zero and from infinity at criticality. What is not proved is the per-pair rate — that τ(0,y) is of order L^{-(d-&alpha)lev y} — although the summed statement it would imply is now a theorem. For the percolation class on the same lattice the pointwise bound is Hutchcroft's. See Critical exponents from the renormalisation group.
    2. Is the threshold a matter of monotonicity? — FKG gives the architecture of the phase diagram and one nearly empty bound on the order parameter; the location of the threshold is the trichotomy's, which is external. See The magnetised phase, and what monotonicity gives.
    3. What does the free energy do at the Ising transition? — The Gaussian case is computed in closed form, including the divergence of the curvature. For Ising spins no per-scale term is elementary and the curvature is computed nowhere. See The free energy and its singularity.
    4. Is the correlation-length exponent known in closed form? — The value is read off the leading eigenvalue of the linearisation; the eigenvalue is known through the expansion in the distance from the marginal exponent, not in closed form. See The Bleher–Sinai fixed point.
    5. Can the logarithms at the upper critical dimension be got right everywhere? — The volume exponent is known in every regime, including the order of the polylogarithmic correction at d = 3α, and that correction disagrees with the one predicted for the Euclidean model; what is not known is the corresponding statement for long-range percolation on Zd. See The volume of the critical cluster.
    6. What is ν for percolation above the triangle threshold? — Below it the classical values hold and the two-point bound pins the exponents; above it the percolation analysis determines everything except the correlation-length exponent. See Renormalisation for hierarchical percolation.
    7. Which criterion holds at the marginal exponent for the scalar model? — Bleher and Major decide the marginal exponent for the vector model and the percolation model has no transition there at all; for the scalar spin model this wiki records no criterion. See The two marginal exponents.
    8. Is there an exact renormalisation group on the Euclidean lattice? — The decimation step produces interactions that depend on the shape of the boundary between blocks, so the recursion is no longer on one variable; the comparison article says what fails. See Hierarchical and Euclidean renormalisation.

    The other direction is worth stating too. Everything marked as a theorem, proposition or corollary in the assertions index is proved in the article that carries it, with the hypotheses in the running text; the attributions are to works, not to theorem numbers, and the figures page says which of the computations are checked by a script.
