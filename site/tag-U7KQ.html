    Cosets, children and the top level, in any block system

    proposition · U7KQ · in Ultrametric block systems and the p-adic models

    Let $n\geq1$. The children of $G_n$ are the $m$ cosets of $G_{n+1}$ in $G_n$, they partition $G_n$, each is a translate of $G_{n+1}$, and if $x,y$ lie in different children of a block $B$ then $\operatorname{lev}(x-y)=\operatorname{lev}(B)$. The same holds with $G_n$ replaced by any coset of it.

    Proof

    Since $G_{n+1}\subseteq G_n$ are subgroups, the cosets of $G_{n+1}$ inside $G_n$ are disjoint and their union is $G_n$; there are $[G_n:G_{n+1}]=m$ of them. A coset $g+G_{n+1}$ with $g\in G_n$ is a translate of $G_{n+1}$. If $x\in g+G_{n+1}$ and $y\in g'+G_{n+1}$ with $g\neq g'$ modulo $G_{n+1}$, then $x-y\in G_n$ because both terms lie in $G_n$, while $x-y\notin G_{n+1}$ because its class in $G_n/G_{n+1}$ is $g-g'\neq0$. Hence the least $k$ with $x-y\in G_k$ is exactly $n$. For a coset $z+G_n$ the same argument applies after translating both points by $-z$, and levels are translation invariant because $G_k$ is a subgroup.

    The proposition is what "the block has no boundary" means algebraically: any two vertices in different children of a block are at the top level of that block, whatever their distance, so a coupling that depends only on the level charges all of them the same rate. In $\mathbb Z_p$ this says that the two balls of radius $p^{-(n+1)}$ inside a ball of radius $p^{-n}$ are joined by edges of one intensity, even though the p-adic distance between a point near the middle of one and a point near the middle of the other is much smaller than $p^{-n}$ — the ultrametric makes every such pair equal.
