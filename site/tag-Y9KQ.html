    Shell averages from the block susceptibility

    proposition · Y9KQ · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Suppose that $\tau_{\beta_c}$ is radial, $\tau_{\beta_c}(0,y)=f(\lVert y\rVert)$, and let $g_k$ be the mean of $f$ over the shell at level $k$. If $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ then $g_k\leq CL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for every $k$, with $C$ depending only on $L,d$ and the constants in the hypothesis; and if $g_k\leq CL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for every $k$ then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\leq A'L^{\kappa n}$. Conversely, if $g_k\asymp L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$, then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$; and if in addition $k\mapsto L^{(d-\kappa)k}g_k$ is monotone, then $\chi_n(\beta_c)\asymp L^{\kappa n}$ implies the two-sided bound $g_k\asymp L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$.

    Proof of the shell estimates

    The shell at level $k$ has $S_k=(1-L^{-d})L^{dk}$ vertices, and $\chi_n=\sum_{k\leq n}S_kg_k$. Since all terms are nonnegative, $S_kg_k\leq\chi_k\leq AL^{\kappa k}$ for $k\leq n$, which is $g_k\leq\frac{A}{1-L^{-d}}L^{-(d-\kappa)k}$; this gives the first two statements, the second by summing the geometric series $\sum_{k\leq n}S_kCL^{-(d-\kappa)k}=C(1-L^{-d})\sum_{k\leq n}L^{\kappa k}$.

    If $g_k\geq cL^{-(d-\kappa)k}$ for all $k$ then the sum is at least a constant times $L^{\kappa n}$, since the last term alone is $S_ng_n\geq c(1-L^{-d})L^{\kappa n}$; this gives the converse. Under the monotonicity hypothesis write $h_k=L^{(d-\kappa)k}g_k$ and note that $\chi_n=\sum_{k\leq n}(1-L^{-d})L^{\kappa k}h_k$; if $h_k\leq h_n$ for $k\leq n$ then $\chi_n\leq(1-L^{-d})h_n\sum_{k\leq n}L^{\kappa k}\leq C'h_nL^{\kappa n}$, so $aL^{\kappa n}\leq\chi_n$ and $\chi_n\leq C'h_nL^{\kappa n}$ give $h_n\geq a/C'$, which is the lower bound; the upper bound is the first statement.

    The second proposition is the version needed for percolation, where the two-point function is known to be radial and the exponent is proved from the block sums. The averaging is essential: the block susceptibility controls the shell means, and the pointwise statement is a separate result, requiring the level-isometry symmetry of the lattice in addition.
