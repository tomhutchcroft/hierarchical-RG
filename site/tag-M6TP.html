    Gaussian two-point function at the top level of a block

    proposition · M6TP · in The Gaussian renormalisation group

    Let $n\geq1$ and let $x,y\in\Lambda_n$ lie in different children of $\Lambda_n$, so that $\lVert x-y\rVert=L^n$. Then in the free-boundary model on $\Lambda_n$,

    $$\tau_\beta(x,y)=\frac{1}{L^{2d(n-1)}}\cdot\frac{b_nv_{n-1}^2}{(1+b_nv_{n-1})\bigl(1-b_nv_{n-1}(m-1)\bigr)}.$$

    Consequently, at $\beta=\beta_c$,

    $$\tau_{\beta_c}(x,y)=\frac{L^{\alpha}-1}{L^{d}-L^{-\alpha}}\,L^{-(d-\alpha)(n-1)},$$

    and for fixed $\beta<\beta_c$, as $n\to\infty$, $\tau_\beta(x,y)\sim\beta J\chi(\beta)^2L^{-(d+\alpha)n}$.

    Proof of the two-point formula

    By the decimation identity the law of $(S_a)$, the child sums of $\Lambda_n$, is the tilt of $\mu_{n-1}^{\otimes m}$ by $\exp(b_n\sum_{a<a'}s_as_{a'})$, so the covariance of two distinct child sums is an off-diagonal entry of the matrix $\Sigma$ of the closure theorem, with $v=v_{n-1}$ and $b=b_n$:

    $$\Sigma_{aa'}=\frac{b_nv_{n-1}^2}{(1+b_nv_{n-1})\bigl(1-b_nv_{n-1}(m-1)\bigr)},\qquad \Sigma_{aa}=\frac{v_{n-1}}{1+b_nv_{n-1}}+\Sigma_{aa'}.$$

    Conditionally on the child sums $(S_a)$, the configuration in the child $a$ is the level-$(n-1)$ Gibbs measure on that child with its total spin prescribed, and by the translation symmetry of a child, $\mathbb E[\sigma_x\mid(S_a)]=S_a/\lvert\Lambda_{n-1}\rvert=S_a/L^{d(n-1)}$ for $x$ in the child $a$. The Hamiltonian factorises over children once the sums are fixed, so the fields in two different children are conditionally independent given $(S_a)$, and $\tau_\beta(x,y)=\mathbb E[S_aS_{a'}]/L^{2d(n-1)}=\Sigma_{aa'}/L^{2d(n-1)}$.

    At $\beta=\beta_c$ one has $b_nv_{n-1}=q:=\frac{1-L^{-\alpha}}{m-1}$ and $v_{n-1}=L^{(d+\alpha)(n-1)}$, so $\Sigma_{aa'}=\frac{qL^{\alpha}}{1+q}v_{n-1}$ and $\tau_{\beta_c}=\frac{qL^\alpha}{1+q}L^{-(d-\alpha)(n-1)}$, with $\frac{qL^\alpha}{1+q}=\frac{L^\alpha-1}{L^d-L^{-\alpha}}$. For $\beta<\beta_c$, $v_{n-1}=\chi L^{d(n-1)}(1+o(1))$ and $b_nv_{n-1}\to0$, so $\Sigma_{aa'}\sim b_nv_{n-1}^2\sim\beta J\chi^2L^{-(d+\alpha)n}L^{2d(n-1)}$.

    The proposition is a statement about the top level of a block at free boundary, and the restriction matters: a pair at level $j$ inside the larger block $\Lambda_n$ with $n>j$ has a larger covariance, because paths inside $\Lambda_n$ that leave the level-$j$ block also join the pair. What is uniform is the asymptotic form, which is read off from the sequence of top levels.

    The two regimes are worth distinguishing. Below criticality the correlation of two adjacent children is of the order of the coupling that joins them, $L^{-(d+\alpha)n}$, which is the kernel itself; the sum over scales then converges and the susceptibility is finite, in agreement with the closed form. At criticality the correlation is larger by a factor $L^{2\alpha n}$ and decays only as $L^{-(d-\alpha)n}$, so the shell sums diverge and the susceptibility with them. The difference between the two exponents is exactly the multiplier: the pair correlation gains $L^{\alpha}$ per scale at criticality, and the modulus $\lVert x-y\rVert^{-(d-\alpha)}$ is the same statement in the variable $\lVert x-y\rVert=L^n$.

    ## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

    One dimension, two children, $\alpha=\tfrac12$. Here $L=2$, $d=1$, $m=2$, $J_k=2^{-3k/2}$ and $\beta_c=2(\sqrt2-1)\approx0.828$. The block-spin variance at criticality is $2^{3n/2}$, and the covariance of two vertices at distance $2^j$ is $0.3204\ldots\times2^{-j/2}$, which is $\lVert x-y\rVert^{-(1-\frac12)}$ up to the constant. The multiplier is $\sqrt2$.

    The free field on $\mathbb Z^d$. Replacing $\mathbb H^d_L$ by $\mathbb Z^d$ with the same power kernel leaves the Gaussian computation meaningful only in the limit of a continuum scaling limit; the block recursion no longer closes, because the block magnetisation is a vector and the interaction between neighbouring blocks depends on their relative position. This is the contrast drawn in the comparison article.

    Non-example: a $\varphi^4$ prior. With $\varrho\propto e^{-\lambda\sigma^4-\frac{\mu}{2}\sigma^2}d\sigma$ the tilted image of a quartic law is not quartic: the summation and the tilt generate all even powers. The recursion leaves the one-parameter family, and the fixed point of the full map is the subject of the Bleher–Sinai article.

    Non-example: the critical field at $\beta=\beta_c$. The formula for $\tau_{\beta_c}$ is a statement about correlations inside a block at free boundary. The infinite-volume Gaussian field does not exist at $\beta_c$, because $I-\beta_cA$ is not invertible on $\ell^2$; the critical object is a scaling limit, and the formula above is the finite-volume shadow of it.

    ## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

    External field. Adding $-\beta h\sum_x\sigma_x$ makes the block-spin law Gaussian with a mean, and the recursion becomes affine: the mean is multiplied by $1/(1+b_nv_{n-1}(m-1))$ per scale. The magnetic eigenvalue of that recursion is the number that appears in $\delta$ for models where the scaling relations hold; in the Gaussian case it reproduces $\delta=\frac{d+\alpha}{d-\alpha}$.

    Any $m$ and $\lambda$. The computation uses only $m$ and $\lambda=L^{d+\alpha}$, so it applies to the operator $\mathcal T_b$ with any integer $m\geq2$ and any coupling ratio $\lambda>m$; with $\alpha=\log(\lambda/m)/\log L$; the multiplier is $\lambda/m=L^{\alpha}$ and the exponents are $\nu=1/\alpha$ and $\kappa=\alpha$, as in the lattice article.

    Complex eigenvalues. The linearisation of the full (non-Gaussian) map at its nontrivial fixed point can have a complex pair of eigenvalues, which produces oscillatory corrections to scaling. In the Gaussian case the linearisation is triangular on the moments and the eigenvalues are real; the phenomenon belongs to the nontrivial fixed point.

    ## History and attribution {#section-history}

    The variance recursion and the identification of the critical point as the value at which the block-spin variance starts to grow like $L^{(d+\alpha)n}$ are in Bleher and Sinai's treatment of Dyson's model [@bleher-sinai-1973], where the Gaussian case is the model problem solved before the general one. The exponent values $\eta=2-\alpha$ and $\nu=1/\alpha$ are the classical long-range values of Fisher, Ma and Nickel [@fisher-ma-nickel-1972], and the role of the Gaussian fixed point as the reference for the renormalisation-group analysis of lattice fields is set out by Bauerschmidt, Brydges and Slade [@bauerschmidt-brydges-slade-2019].
