    Koch and Wittwer: the large-field problem

    reading map · source-koch-wittwer

    This is the reading map for the three papers in which Hans Koch and Peter Wittwer proved that the hierarchical renormalisation map has a nontrivial fixed point with unbounded spins. The group is small and unusually homogeneous: the same two authors, one object, four papers, and a method that is the reason the papers are cited. The method is not a numerical certification. It is an analytic treatment of the large-field region, the part of the integral defining the effective potential in which the spin is too large for the perturbative expansion to converge and which, for an unbounded single-spin distribution, carries a non-negligible part of the measure. Borel summation resums the series there and brings the region back inside a convergent expansion.

    The point of the group, for this wiki, is that it closes the gap between the two other general treatments: the theorem of Bleher and Sinai gives existence in a class of measures with a qualitative statement and a parameter range, and the epsilon expansion of Gawedzki and Kupiainen describes a neighbourhood of the marginal exponent. Koch and Wittwer prove the fixed point of the hierarchical approximation of the three-dimensional theory, with convergent expansions around it, without a small parameter.

    What is taken from each

    The 1986 paper

    The result the wiki states is the existence of the fixed point with convergent expansions around it, for unbounded spins, in the hierarchical approximation of the three-dimensional theory. The mechanism the wiki describes is the one the paper's own summary gives: the large-field region is the obstacle, Borel summation is what removes it, and the technique is an improvement of the analyticity methods of Gawedzki and Kupiainen's hierarchy-model papers. The wiki does not attempt to reproduce the estimates, and it separates two claims that the literature sometimes runs together: the existence of the fixed point, which is this paper, and the one-dimensional instability that makes it critical, which is the general theory and the companion paper.

    The 1988 note

    This is where the computer-assisted element of the group's work belongs: an outline of a proof, in an extended Wilson-Kadanoff scheme, that gives rigorous bounds on the exponent governing the scaling of the free energy density for a one-parameter family of hierarchical systems in three dimensions. The wiki cites it for the bounds and for the separation of two questions that are easily run together: the fixed point exists by the analysis of 1986, and the exponents at it are bounded by the computation described here. The exponent in the note is the free-energy exponent, which is two minus the specific-heat exponent in the notation of this wiki.

    The 1991 paper

    Used for two things. First, the renormalisation transformation is studied as an operator, with its beta function and asymptotics, which is the form in which the wiki's map article presents it. Second, the paper connects Dyson's hierarchy with Gallavotti's: the two differ in the block structure and agree in the form of the map. That connection is the reason the wiki treats the hierarchies as one family rather than as unrelated models, and it is the natural bridge to the Dyson-Baker variant of 1994.

    The 1994 paper

    The Dyson-Baker hierarchy replaces the ultrametric blocks by the blocks of an expanding map of the interval. The paper proves a nontrivial fixed point there, which is what makes the existence a property of the class of block structures rather than of one arithmetic choice of lattice.

    Outside this map

    Koch's renormalisation work on area-preserving maps and on the breakup of invariant tori belongs to KAM theory rather than to the statistical-mechanical renormalisation group, and the wiki does not use it. Wittwer's work on the Euclidean fourth-order model in three dimensions is the Euclidean counterpart of the subject, and it is cited in this wiki only through the surveys that place it next to the hierarchical analysis. Background rather than part of the group: the analyticity techniques the 1986 method improves on, Gawedzki and Kupiainen's hierarchy-model paper of 1982 and their Borel-summability paper with Tirozzi of 1984; and the general existence theorem of Bleher and Sinai, whose hypotheses the present result tightens for this model.

    What is not claimed

    No locator is claimed, and no estimate is reproduced. The statements attributed to the 1986 paper follow its published summary; those attributed to the 1991 and 1994 papers follow their titles and subject classifications, since their summaries are withheld from the zbMATH API for licensing reasons. The wiki cites the three works by work and not by theorem number.
