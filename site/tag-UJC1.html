    The growth exponent is alpha, in the sense that the normalised variance cannot decay exponentially

    theorem · UJC1 · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Let $\chi_n=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n}\tau(0,y)$ be the block susceptibility, and recall the mean-field lower bound of the sharpness theory for ferromagnetic Ising models: there is $c>0$ with $\chi(\beta)\geq c(\beta_c-\beta)^{-1}$ for $\beta<\beta_c$, whence $\chi_n(\beta_c)$ is unbounded in $n$. Then, at $\beta=\beta_c$,

    $$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\chi_n}{n\log L}=\alpha,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log V_n}{n\log L}=d+\alpha.$$
    Proof

    By the block identity, $V_n=m^n\chi_n$, so $R_n=\chi_nL^{-\alpha n}$ and the two statements are equivalent. The upper bound of the previous theorem gives $\limsup\leq\alpha$. Suppose the limit superior were smaller, say $R_n\leq DL^{-\varepsilon n}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ and all $n$. Then $R_n\to0$, so the tilt parameter $\beta_cJ_nV_{n-1}=cR_{n-1}$ is small and (1) can be expanded: the tilted pair law is a small perturbation of the product law, giving

    $$V_n=mV_{n-1}\bigl[1+(m-1)\beta J_nV_{n-1}+O\bigl((\beta J_nV_{n-1})^2\bigr)\bigr],$$

    whose logarithm is $\log\chi_n-\log\chi_{n-1}=O(R_{n-1})=O(L^{-\varepsilon n})$. Those increments are summable, so $\chi_n$ would be bounded, contradicting the unboundedness. Hence the limit superior is at least $\alpha$, and with the upper bound it is $\alpha$.

    The expansion step is legitimate because the rescaled child law has a variance proxy of one and a sub-Gaussian moment generating function uniformly in $n$ and $\beta$ — which is the GHS inequality integrated three times — so the exponential tilt is analytic in its parameter on a fixed disc and the second-order expansion is uniform. What the theorem does not give is the amplitude: it excludes exponential decay of $R_n$ but says nothing about polynomial decay, so $\liminf R_n>0$ is not claimed.

    ### What is still open {#section-amplitude}

    Two things, and they are the same thing seen twice: the amplitude bound $R_n\geq c>0$ is not proved, and neither is the pointwise decay $\tau_\infty(0,y)\leq AL^{-(d-\alpha)\operatorname{lev}(y)}$ which would imply it. For the percolation class on the same lattice both that pointwise bound and a matching finite-volume lower bound are theorems, as the volume article and the percolation article record. The two statements that a pointwise route needs are recorded here, because between them they give the amplitude and because the first is a monotonicity that does not follow from FKG.
