    At criticality the normalised second moment is bounded away from zero and from infinity

    theorem · UJC1 · in Critical exponents from the renormalisation group

    Let $R_n=V_n/L^{(d+\alpha)n}$ and $\beta=\beta_c$. Assume the mean-field lower bound for the susceptibility of the sharpness theory, as above, so that $\chi_n(\beta_c)$ is unbounded in $n$. Then there are $0<c\leq K<\infty$, depending only on $L,d,\alpha$, with

    $$c\ \leq\ R_n\ \leq\ K\qquad\text{for every }n\geq0,$$

    so $\kappa=\alpha$, the rescaled block spin has a second moment bounded away from zero and infinity, and in particular $\lim_n\frac{\log V_n}{n\log L}=d+\alpha$.

    Proof

    Write $\rho=L^{-\alpha}<1$ and $q=(1+\rho)/2<1$. The upper bound is the previous theorem. For the lower one, the tilt of a block is small whenever $R_{n-1}$ is: the tilt parameter is $\beta_cJ_nV_{n-1}=c_0R_{n-1}$ with $c_0=\beta_c/r$, and the child law has a sub-Gaussian moment generating function uniformly in the scale, by GHS integrated three times. The tilted pair law is therefore an analytic perturbation of the product law on a disc whose radius does not depend on $n$, and the expansion of the previous section is two-sided: there is $r_0>0$ with

    $$R_{n-1}\leq r_0\ \Longrightarrow\ R_n\leq q\,R_{n-1}\leq r_0.$$

    Indeed $R_n=\rho R_{n-1}\bigl(1+O(R_{n-1})\bigr)$ with the error uniform, and $\rho(1+\varepsilon)\leq q$ once $\varepsilon\leq(1-\rho)/(2\rho)$, which a small enough $r_0$ ensures. So if some $R_N\leq r_0$, induction gives $R_{N+j}\leq q^jR_N$, the region is never left, and $\sum_nR_n<\infty$. But $\log\chi_n-\log\chi_{n-1}=\log\bigl(L^{\alpha}R_n/R_{n-1}\bigr)=O(R_{n-1})$ once $R_{n-1}$ is small, so the increments of $\log\chi_n$ would be summable beyond $N$ and $\chi_n$ would be bounded — contradicting the assumed unboundedness. Hence no $R_N$ lies below $r_0$, and $R_n\geq r_0$ for every $n$; since $R_0=1$ this gives the lower bound with $c=\min\{1,r_0\}$.

    The same argument shows more than the boxed statement: it shows that the sequence $R_n$ cannot decay at all, so the exponential rate of $V_n$ is exactly $d+\alpha$ rather than merely at most it. The two halves are complementary and neither uses the convergence of the rescaled orbit: the pair (upper, amplitude) is what the growth theorem previously needed as a hypothesis, and it is now a conclusion.
