<p>Every identity in <a href="/articles/hierarchical-gibbs-measure/">the decimation article</a> is finite-volume and exact, and that is deliberate: the renormalisation group acts on the law of a block spin of a block, and a block never needs the lattice. But the objects the rest of the wiki measures are not finite-volume objects. The two-point function $\tau_\beta(x,y)$ of <a href="/articles/critical-exponents-from-rg/">the exponent article</a>, the phase that the intensity selects, and the statement that $\tau_\beta$ depends on the level alone are all statements about a measure on the whole lattice, and the lattice is infinite.</p>

<p>This article supplies the passage. Three things are proved and nothing more is assumed: that the finite-volume measures have limit points, that every limit point satisfies the defining property of a Gibbs measure — the conditional law of a finite region given the rest of the configuration, which is the one condition that survives the limit — and that a limit point of the free-boundary measures inherits the lattice symmetry, so its two-point function is radial. What the limit <em>is</em>, whether it is unique, and whether the model is magnetised are questions of a different order: the last section of this article says which of them this argument does not touch, and <a href="/articles/hierarchical-magnetised-phase/">the next one</a> answers them for ferromagnetic couplings, in the form of the sign of a single number.</p>

<p>The passage needs one arithmetic input, and it is the reason the article belongs to the first part. The coupling from a block to its complement, summed over the complement, is the same number for every vertex of the block — the lattice gives each vertex of $\Lambda_n$ exactly the same view of the outside — and that number decays like $L^{-\alpha n}$ while its sum over the block grows or decays according to $\alpha-d$. The first fact is what makes the specification continuous; the second says when the boundary is thermodynamically large.</p>

## The specification {#section-specification}

<p>Spins are attached to the vertices of $\mathbb H^d_L$, so a configuration is a point $\sigma=(\sigma_x)_{x\in\mathbb H^d_L}$ of $\Omega=\{\pm1\}^{\mathbb H^d_L}$, and $\sigma_x$ is the coordinate map. For a finite set $A$ let $\mathcal F_A$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\{\sigma_x:x\in A\}$; a function is <em>local</em> if it is bounded and $\mathcal F_A$-measurable for some finite $A$. The Hamiltonian $H_\Lambda$ and the prior $\varrho$ are those of <a href="/articles/hierarchical-gibbs-measure/#section-model">the decimation article</a>.</p>
<a id="tag-R5T3" data-assertion="definition" data-title="Boundary field, finite-volume Gibbs measure, and the DLR property"></a>
<p>A <em>boundary field</em> on a finite $\Lambda$ is a function $h:\Lambda\to\mathbb R$, and the finite-volume measure with boundary field $h$ is</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P_\Lambda^h(d\sigma)=\frac{1}{Z_\Lambda(h)}\exp\Bigl(-\beta H_\Lambda(\sigma)+\beta\sum_{x\in\Lambda}h_x\sigma_x\Bigr)\prod_{x\in\Lambda}\varrho(d\sigma_x).$$</div>
<p>Two cases are used. The <em>free</em> boundary condition is $h\equiv0$, which is the measure $\mathbb P_\Lambda$ of the decimation article. A <em>fixed</em> boundary condition is a configuration $\omega$, and the field it induces is $h_x=\sum_{y\notin\Lambda}J(x-y)\omega_y$; the resulting measure is the model on $\Lambda$ with the outside held at $\omega$, since the omitted energy is $-\sum_{x\in\Lambda,y\notin\Lambda}J(x-y)\sigma_x\omega_y$ and is linear in $\sigma$ on $\Lambda$. For finite $A\subset\Lambda$ and a field $h$ on $A$ write $\gamma_A^h$ for the law of $\sigma_A$ with density $\exp(-\beta H_A(\sigma_A)+\beta\sum_{x\in A}h_x\sigma_x)$ against the prior: the <em>Gibbs conditional</em> of $A$ in the field $h$. With $h_A(\omega)_x=\sum_{y\notin A}J(x-y)\omega_y$, a probability measure $\mu$ on $\Omega$ is a <em>Gibbs measure</em> if</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb E_\mu\bigl[f\mid\mathcal F_{A^c}\bigr](\omega)=\int f\,d\gamma_A^{h_A(\omega)}\qquad\mu\text{-a.e.}$$</div>
<p>for every finite $A$ and every bounded local $f$ on $A$.</p>
<p>The sums defining $h_A(\omega)$ converge absolutely because $\omega$ is bounded and $\sum_{y}\lvert J(x-y)\rvert<\infty$, which is the <a href="/articles/hierarchical-lattice/#section-kernels">summability criterion of the lattice article</a>; without it the conditional law in the definition would not exist, and the last section of examples is built on that. The condition is the one of Dobrushin and of Lanford and Ruelle, and it says that the measure is compatible with all of its own one-region conditional laws at once [@dobrushin-1968] [@lanford-ruelle-1969]. Nothing in it refers to a volume or to a boundary, which is why it is the right notion for a limit.</p>

## The boundary field of a block {#section-boundary}

<p>The hierarchical lattice makes one computation exact, because a block is a subgroup: every vertex of $\Lambda_n$ sees the complement in the same way.</p>
<a id="tag-AHO7" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="The coupling of a block to its complement, and the volume it subtends"></a>
<p>Let $n\geq0$ and $x\in\Lambda_n$. Then</p>
<div class="math-display">$$B_n:=\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_n}\lvert J(x-y)\rvert=J(1-L^{-d})\frac{L^{-\alpha(n+1)}}{1-L^{-\alpha}},$$</div>
<p>independently of $x\in\Lambda_n$, and in particular $B_n\to0$. The total coupling between $\Lambda_n$ and its complement is $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$, and</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n=\frac{J(1-L^{-d})L^{-\alpha}}{1-L^{-\alpha}}\,L^{(d-\alpha)n}\ \longrightarrow\ \begin{cases}0,&\alpha>d,\\ \infty,&\alpha<d.\end{cases}$$</div>
:::details Proof
<p>Let $x\in\Lambda_n$ and $y\notin\Lambda_n$, so that $x_i=0$ for $i>n$ and $y$ has some coordinate above $n$ different from $0$. Then $x-y$ vanishes above the largest index $m$ with $y_m\neq0$, and does not vanish there, so $\operatorname{lev}(x-y)=\operatorname{lev}(y)$: the level of the pair is the level of $y$ as a vertex, exactly when $x$ is in the block below $y$. Hence $J(x-y)=J_{\operatorname{lev}(y)}$, and summing over the shell at level $j$ gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$B_n=\sum_{j\geq n+1}\bigl(L^{dj}-L^{d(j-1)}\bigr)JL^{-(d+\alpha)j}=J(1-L^{-d})\sum_{j\geq n+1}L^{-\alpha j}=\frac{J(1-L^{-d})L^{-\alpha(n+1)}}{1-L^{-\alpha}},$$</div>
<p>using the shell counts of <a href="/articles/hierarchical-lattice/#section-blocks">the lattice article</a> and the geometric series, which converges because $\alpha>0$. The total coupling is $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ with $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert=L^{dn}$, and $L^{dn}L^{-\alpha(n+1)}=L^{-\alpha}L^{(d-\alpha)n}$ gives the dichotomy.</p>
:::
<div class="fig" data-fig="boundary" data-title="The coupling of a block to its complement, for three exponents, on a logarithmic scale. The dashed curves are the field one vertex feels, which decays at rate L to the minus alpha for every exponent; the solid curves are the same number summed over the block, which grows below alpha = d, is flat at alpha = d, and decays above it."></div>
<p>Both statements are about the same number, and they pull in opposite directions. Per vertex the boundary is negligible for every $\alpha>0$: that is the hypothesis the Gibbs property needs, and it holds in the whole range of the model. The block totals it, and the cross energy $-\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n,y\notin\Lambda_n}J(x-y)\sigma_x\sigma_y$ has modulus at most $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ with each pair counted once from the block's side, so $\beta\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n$ — the largest exponent the boundary can contribute — diverges exactly when $\alpha&lt;d$. So in the long-range range the boundary of a block carries unbounded energy while no single vertex feels more than $B_n$, and that is the precise sense in which the model is long-range: not that a spin is strongly coupled to the outside, but that there are too many outside spins. For $\alpha>d$ the cross energy stays bounded, and the boundary is a finite-volume effect.</p>
<p>Neither case obstructs the limit. The next two sections take the limit with only $B_n\to0$, and the dichotomy is recorded because it is what distinguishes the two ranges of the model, and because the marginal value $\alpha=d$ — the value at which the cross energy neither vanishes nor diverges — is where the model's own criteria become delicate, as the <a href="/articles/hierarchical-vector-models/#section-thouless">vector article</a> records.</p>

## The conditional law is continuous in the boundary {#section-continuity}

<p>Everything below is a consequence of one Lipschitz estimate, which says that changing the boundary field changes the conditional law of a finite region by at most the size of the change.</p>
<a id="tag-KS0Z" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="The Gibbs conditional is Lipschitz in the boundary field"></a>
<p>Let $A$ be finite, let $h,h'$ be fields on $A$, and let $c=\beta\sum_{x\in A}\lvert h_x-h'_x\rvert$. Then the laws $\gamma_A^h$ and $\gamma_A^{h'}$ satisfy $\lVert\gamma_A^h-\gamma_A^{h'}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\tanh(c/2)\leq c/2$, and consequently for every bounded local $f$ on $A$</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\Bigl\lvert\int f\,d\gamma_A^h-\int f\,d\gamma_A^{h'}\Bigr\rvert\leq 2\lVert f\rVert_\infty\tanh(c/2)\leq\beta\lVert f\rVert_\infty\sum_{x\in A}\lvert h_x-h'_x\rvert.$$</div>
<p>Two consequences are used. First, $\omega\mapsto\gamma_A^{h_A(\omega)}$ is continuous in the product topology: each $h_A(\omega)_x=\sum_{y\notin A}J(x-y)\omega_y$ is a uniform limit of functions of finitely many coordinates, hence continuous, because $\sum_y\lvert J(x-y)\rvert<\infty$ and $\lvert\omega\rvert\leq1$. Second, for $A\subset\Lambda_n$ the conditional of the free-boundary measure differs from the infinite-volume conditional by at most</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\Bigl\lVert\gamma_A^{h_{A|\Lambda_n}(\sigma)}-\gamma_A^{h_A(\sigma)}\Bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\tanh\Bigl(\tfrac12\beta\lvert A\rvert B_n\Bigr),$$</div>
<p>where $h_{A|\Lambda_n}(x)=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n\setminus A}J(x-y)\sigma_y$ uses only the spins inside the block, as the free boundary condition prescribes.</p>
:::details Proof
<p>Write $\Lambda=A$. The two densities against the prior differ only by the factor $\exp\bigl(\beta\sum_{x\in A}\sigma_x(h_x-h'_x)\bigr)$, which lies in $[e^{-c},e^{c}]$ because $\lvert\sigma_x\rvert\leq1$. If two probability densities have ratio everywhere in $[a,b]$, their total variation distance is at most $(b-a)/(b+a)$: the positive part of the difference is at most $\tfrac12\int p\,(b-a)/(b+a)$ in the normalised form, which is the bound. With $b/a=e^{c}$ this is $\tanh(c/2)$, and $\tanh u\leq u$ since $(\tanh u)'=\operatorname{sech}^2u\leq1$ and $\tanh0=0$. Expectations differ by at most $2\lVert f\rVert_\infty$ times the total variation distance.</p>
<p>For the second consequence, the two fields on $A$ agree on the contribution of $y\in\Lambda_n\setminus A$ and differ only in the contribution of $y\notin\Lambda_n$, which is at most $B_n$ in modulus at each $x\in A$ by <a href="/tag/AHO7/">the boundary-field proposition</a>. So $c\leq\beta\lvert A\rvert B_n$ and the estimate applies.</p>
<p>For the continuity, fix $x$ and $N$; the partial sum $\sum_{\lVert y\rVert\leq L^N}J(x-y)\omega_y$ depends on finitely many coordinates, and the remainder is at most $\sum_{\lVert y\rVert>L^N}\lvert J(x-y)\rvert\to0$ uniformly in $\omega$. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, and a finite tuple of continuous fields gives a conditional law that depends continuously on them by the estimate above, so $\omega\mapsto\gamma_A^{h_A(\omega)}$ is continuous.</p>
:::
<p>The estimate is quantitative where it matters: the error in the conditional is of order $\beta\lvert A\rvert B_n$, so it is small uniformly in the configuration and does not degrade as the volume grows. That uniformity is the whole content of the theorem below, and it is what fails when the interaction is not summable.</p>

## Existence, and the Gibbs property {#section-existence}

<a id="tag-74TC" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Limit points of the finite-volume measures are Gibbs measures"></a>
<p>Let $\alpha>0$, let $\lvert\sigma_x\rvert\leq1$, and let $(\Lambda_n)_{n\geq0}$ be the exhausting sequence of blocks. Then:</p>
<p>(i) the set of limit points in the weak topology of the free-boundary measures $(\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n})_{n\geq0}$ is nonempty, and every limit point is a Gibbs measure;</p>
<p>(ii) for any fixed configuration $\omega$, every limit point of the measures $(\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega})_{n\geq0}$ with boundary condition $\omega$ is a Gibbs measure.</p>
:::details Proof
<p><em>Compactness.</em> $\Omega=\{\pm1\}^{\mathbb H^d_L}$ is a product of compact spaces, hence compact, and metrisable because the index set is countable; its Borel probability measures are weakly compact. So every sequence has a limit point, and the set of limit points of a sequence is nonempty. This is where the boundedness of the spins enters: for $\varrho=N(0,1)$ the space is not compact and tightness has to be supplied separately.</p>
<p><em>Conditioning.</em> Let $f$ be bounded and local on a finite $A$, and $g$ bounded and local on $A^c$; both are continuous. For $n$ with $A\subset\Lambda_n$, the tower property in the finite volume gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega}}[fg]=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}^{\omega}}\bigl[\gamma_{A|{\Lambda_n}}(f)\,g\bigr],$$</div>
<p>where $\gamma_{A|{\Lambda_n}}(f)(\sigma)=\int f\,d\gamma_A^{h}$ with the field $h_x=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n\setminus A}J(x-y)\sigma_y$ on the spins of the block outside $A$.</p>
<p><em>Case (ii).</em> With the fixed boundary condition $\omega$, the spins outside $\Lambda_n$ are $\omega$, so $h_x=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_n\setminus A}J(x-y)\sigma_y+\sum_{y\notin\Lambda_n}J(x-y)\omega_y$ and therefore $\gamma_{A|{\Lambda_n}}(f)=\gamma_A^{h_A(\cdot)}(f)$ exactly, as functions on $\Omega$: the two fields are the same field. This function is continuous and bounded by $\lVert f\rVert_\infty$, so along any weakly convergent subsequence $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n_k}}^{\omega}\to\mu$ both sides converge, giving $\mathbb E_\mu[fg]=\mathbb E_\mu[g\,\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)]$ for all such $f,g$, which is the Gibbs property.</p>
<p><em>Case (i).</em> Here the block outside $A$ is free, so the field of the conditional is $h_{A|\Lambda_n}$ and the second consequence of <a href="/tag/KS0Z/">the continuity proposition</a> applies: $\gamma_{A|{\Lambda_n}}(f)\to\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)$ uniformly, with error at most $2\lVert f\rVert_\infty\tanh(\beta\lvert A\rvert B_n/2)\to0$. Each $\gamma_{A|{\Lambda_n}}(f)$ is a function of finitely many spins, hence continuous, and bounded by $\lVert f\rVert_\infty$. So for a weakly convergent subsequence $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n_k}}\to\mu$, the left side tends to $\mathbb E_\mu[fg]$ and the right side to $\mathbb E_\mu[g\,\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)]$, and the two are equal. Since $f$ and $A$ were arbitrary and the local functions on $A^c$ generate $\mathcal F_{A^c}$, this is the Gibbs property.</p>
<p>Note where the argument cannot be shortcut: $\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)$ depends on the whole outside configuration and is not continuous, so it cannot be tested directly against a weak limit. The proof passes through the finite-volume conditionals, which are continuous because they see finitely many spins, and uses the uniformity of their convergence to replace them.</p>
:::
<p>Two remarks on the statement. The measures in (ii) with different $\omega$ have different limits in general, and the theorem asserts only that each is Gibbs; the Gibbs property constrains the conditional laws of a measure and does not compare two measures. And the proof of (i) uses nothing about $\Lambda_n$ beyond the fact that the blocks exhaust the lattice and that the coupling of $\Lambda_n$ to its complement decays at each vertex, so it applies to any exhaustion of any block system in which each vertex has finite total intensity — which is the form in which <a href="/articles/ultrametric-block-systems/">the general article</a> states the lattice.</p>

## The limit is symmetric {#section-invariance}

<p>The finite-volume measures are not invariant under anything, since a block has a boundary, but a limit point is, because every symmetry of the lattice eventually preserves every block.</p>
<a id="tag-ZR91" data-assertion="corollary" data-title="Limit points of the free-boundary measures are invariant, so their two-point function is radial"></a>
<p>Let $\mu$ be a limit point of $(\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n})_{n\geq0}$. Then $\mu$ is invariant under every translation of $\mathbb H^d_L$ and under every coordinate-wise bijection, and consequently</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\tau_\mu(x,y):=\mathbb E_\mu[\sigma_x\sigma_y]=\tau_\mu(0,y-x),$$</div>
<p>depends on $\operatorname{lev}(y-x)$ alone. Moreover the block sums of the children of any block are exchangeable under $\mu$.</p>
:::details Proof
<p>Let $\psi$ be a translation by $v\in\mathbb H^d_L$ or a coordinate-wise bijection. For $n\geq\operatorname{lev}(v)$ in the first case and every $n$ in the second, $\psi$ maps $\Lambda_n$ onto itself, and it preserves the level of every pair, so it preserves the Hamiltonian $H_{\Lambda_n}$ and the prior; hence $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}$ is invariant under $\psi$. For a bounded local $f$ and a weakly convergent subsequence $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n_k}}\to\mu$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb E_\mu[f]=\lim_k\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n_k}}}[f]=\lim_k\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_{n_k}}}[f\circ\psi]=\mathbb E_\mu[f\circ\psi],$$</div>
<p>because $n_k\to\infty$ and the invariance holds from some index on. So $\mu$ is invariant under both families. Invariance under the translations gives $\tau_\mu(x,y)=\tau_\mu(0,y-x)$, and invariance under the coordinate-wise bijections, together with the transitivity of <a href="/articles/hierarchical-symmetries/#section-isometries">the isometry theorem</a> on pairs at a fixed level, gives that $\tau_\mu(0,z)$ depends only on $\operatorname{lev}(z)$. Exchangeability of the child sums is statement (ii) of the same article, applied to $\mu$.</p>
:::
<p>This is the statement the rest of the wiki needs. <a href="/articles/critical-exponents-from-rg/#section-identities">The exponent article</a> passes from the susceptibility, an average over a shell, to the pointwise two-point function, and it does so for a measure that is known to be radial; the corollary is what makes the free-boundary limit such a measure. It is not a statement about every Gibbs measure: a Gibbs measure selected by a magnetised boundary condition is not translation invariant in the same way, and the corollary is about the symmetric limit, which is the one the recursion produces.</p>

## Examples and non-examples {#section-examples}

<p><em>No interaction.</em> For $J=0$ the finite-volume measures are the product of the prior, and $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}=\varrho^{\otimes\Lambda_n}$ has the product measure $\varrho^{\otimes\mathbb H^d_L}$ as its limit; the Gibbs property holds with $\gamma_A$ the product law, and the limit is invariant. This is the only case in which the limit is read off without an argument.</p>

<p><em>A kernel that is not summable at a vertex.</em> Take $\alpha=0$, that is $J_k=L^{-dk}$, so that each vertex is coupled to every level at the same total rate. Then the sum over the $j$-th shell is $(1-L^{-d})J$ for every $j$, the boundary field $B_n$ diverges with $n$, and the series defining $h_A(\omega)$ diverges. The energy of a configuration is not defined, no conditional law exists, and the model is not a Gibbs system at all. The hypothesis $\alpha>0$ is therefore not technical: it is exactly summability at a vertex, and it fails at $\alpha=0$ and at no other value of the exponent of this family.</p>

<p><em>The long-range range is not a non-example.</em> For $0<\alpha<d$ the limit exists and is Gibbs by the theorem, although the cross energy of a block diverges by <a href="/tag/AHO7/">the boundary-field proposition</a>. The two facts are compatible because they are about different quantities: the theorem needs the per-vertex field to vanish, and the divergence is a statement about how many vertices there are. This is the resolution of the gap that <a href="/articles/hierarchical-gibbs-measure/#section-variants">the decimation article</a> left open.</p>

<p><em>Gaussian spins.</em> For $\varrho=N(0,1)$ the spins are unbounded and neither half of the theorem applies as stated. Compactness becomes tightness: if $\sup_n\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}}[\sigma_0^2]<\infty$ then the sequence is tight and limit points exist; the conditional law is still Lipschitz in the field on the set where the outside configuration is bounded, which is where the uniform convergence of the fields holds, so the Gibbs property follows on that set and hence everywhere under the tightness assumption. Both statements are assumptions here and are not proved in this article: what is proved is the bounded-spin case, and what is needed for the unbounded one is stated.</p>

<p><em>Not a Gibbs system: percolation.</em> Bond percolation on the same lattice is not a spin system and has no boundary field of this kind: the state of a bond is not the sum of local energies, and the object that plays the role of the specification is the connectivity event. <a href="/articles/hierarchical-percolation-rg/">The percolation article</a> takes the infinite-volume limit of the connection probabilities directly, which is a different construction, and the two should not be conflated.</p>

## Variants and generalisations {#section-variants}

<p><em>The set of Gibbs measures.</em> It is convex, because the Gibbs property is an identity between integrals that is preserved by convex combinations, and it is closed in the weak topology: if $\mu_k\to\mu$ and each $\mu_k$ is Gibbs, then for bounded local $f$ on $A$ and $g$ on $A^c$ the identity $\mathbb E_{\mu_k}[fg]=\mathbb E_{\mu_k}[g\,\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)]$ passes to the limit, since $g\,\gamma_A^{h_A}(f)$ is continuous and bounded. In the compact case it is therefore a nonempty compact convex set, and a Gibbs measure that is not its only point is a phase: convex combinations of two distinct Gibbs measures are again Gibbs, so the set of phases is the set of extreme points.</p>

<p><em>An external field.</em> Adding $-h\sum_x\sigma_x$ to the Hamiltonian adds $\beta h$ to each field $h_x$ in the specification, and the boundary-field computation is unchanged since the field is constant. The triviality of this modification is the reason the recursion treats the field as a separate argument in <a href="/articles/renormalisation-group-map/">the map article</a>.</p>

<p><em>Other block systems.</em> Nothing in the argument uses more than that the blocks exhaust the vertex set and that $\sum_y\lvert J(x-y)\rvert<\infty$; the exact formula for $B_n$ uses that every vertex of a block has the same view of the complement, which is a property of any block system whose blocks are cosets of nested subgroups. On $\mathbb Z^d$ with a finite-range interaction the same theorem holds and is classical; on $\mathbb Z^d$ with a power-law interaction the per-vertex sum converges exactly when the exponent exceeds the dimension, which is the condition under which the model is a Gibbs system at all.</p>

<p><em>What the limit does to the recursion.</em> The decimation identity is a statement about $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}$ and holds at every $n$; a limit point $\mu$ does not inherit it as an identity, because a block of an infinite lattice has a complement to be coupled to. What $\mu$ does inherit is the symmetry of the corollary, and the laws of the block sums $S_{\Lambda_n}$ under $\mathbb P_{\Lambda_n}$ — the finite-volume objects of the recursion — converge whenever the measures do, along the same subsequence. The recursion is thus the right object at finite volume, and the limit is the right object for the exponents, and the two are connected by the fact that the exponents are read off the growth of the $S_{\Lambda_n}$ precisely along this limit.</p>

## History and attribution {#section-history}

<p>The formulation of a Gibbs measure by its conditional laws is due to Dobrushin [@dobrushin-1968] and to Lanford and Ruelle [@lanford-ruelle-1969], and the general theory, including the statement that limit points of finite-volume measures are Gibbs measures when the specification is continuous, is the content of the first chapters of Georgii's book [@georgii-2011]. The hierarchical model was introduced by Dyson and analysed by Bleher and Sinai, who work with the finite-volume measures and the recursion and identify the critical point as the boundary between two domains of attraction [@bleher-sinai-1973]; the passage to the infinite volume in that literature is made with the compactness argument of this article and is not usually separated from the analysis that follows it.</p>

<p>The computation of the boundary field is elementary and is the same count as the summability criterion for the kernel; the reason to record it separately is that it separates two conditions that are easy to confuse. Summability at a vertex, which holds for every $\alpha>0$, is what the definition of a Gibbs measure needs; the growth rate $\lvert\Lambda_n\rvert B_n\asymp L^{(d-\alpha)n}$ is what decides whether the boundary is thermodynamically relevant, and it is the arithmetic behind the distinction between the mean-field and non-mean-field ranges in the rest of the wiki. The corollary on invariance is the infinite-volume form of the symmetry statements of <a href="/articles/hierarchical-symmetries/">the isometry article</a>.</p>
