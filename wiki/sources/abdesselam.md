# Abdesselam: from one fixed point to another

This is the reading map for Abdesselam's work on trajectories of the renormalisation group. The
group is small and its object is unusual: not a fixed point and not a local analysis around one,
but a whole orbit of the exact renormalisation group, defined at every scale, joining the
ultraviolet Gaussian fixed point to a nontrivial infrared one. The construction is
nonperturbative, it does not use the hierarchical approximation, and its unperturbed problem is
nevertheless a one-dimensional iteration of the kind the hierarchical model supplies - which is
why it belongs in this wiki.

## What is taken from each

### The 2007 paper: the trajectory

The result is a sequence of effective potentials, one per scale, each the exact image of the
previous one under the renormalisation step, asymptotic to the Gaussian fixed point in the
ultraviolet and to the nontrivial fixed point in the infrared. The wiki states this as the theorem
of the trajectory article, with the two endpoints and the exactness of the recursion as its
content, and with the modified propagator recorded as a hypothesis rather than suppressed: the
trajectory is a statement about the modified model.

What the wiki does not do is reproduce the estimates. The mechanism is described in the terms the
account of the construction uses - a one-dimensional unperturbed problem, a small perturbation
supplied by the propagator, and a weighted space of sequences in which the recursion is a
contraction - and the norms themselves are left to the paper.

### The 2009 account: the method

This is where the relation to the hierarchical model is stated in the form the wiki uses: the
renormalisation group map is defined rigorously and nonperturbatively without the hierarchical
approximation, and the trajectories are constructed by perturbing a nonlinear one-dimensional
iteration. That sentence is the quantitative version of the comparison drawn in the article on
hierarchical and Euclidean renormalisation, and the wiki cites this account for it.

### The 2018 programme: why the ultrametric case matters

If a three-dimensional conformal field theory is to be constructed by renormalisation-group
methods, the setting in which the block structure is exact is the natural testing ground. The
paper develops the ultrametric and p-adic versions of the model with that end in view. The wiki
uses it only for the variants: the p-adic and ultrametric models as members of the same class, and
the reason the class is worth having.

## Outside this map

Abdesselam's cluster and tree expansions - the botanical garden of trees, forests and jungles; the
explicit large versus small field multiscale cluster expansion; the bosonic monocluster expansion
with Magnen and Rivasseau - are the analytic machinery of multiscale analysis rather than
statements about hierarchical models, and the wiki does not use them. His work on the Jacobian
conjecture and on the Lagrange-Good inversion formula is outside the subject entirely.

Background rather than part of the map: the nontrivial infrared fixed point of the modified
three-dimensional model, constructed by Brydges, Mitter and Scoppola, and the potential
formulation of the renormalisation group of Bauerschmidt, Brydges and Slade, which the wiki uses
for the definition of the step that the trajectory is an orbit of.

## What is not claimed

No locator is claimed: the wiki cites these works by work, not by theorem number. The trajectory is
a statement about the model with modified propagator, and the wiki does not claim it for the
unmodified three-dimensional phi-fourth theory, whose renormalisation-group trajectory is
constructed by different means. A zbMATH search under this author's code returns exactly the two
renormalisation-group papers above, together with the 2018 programme; the map is therefore complete
for this subject.
