# Bleher, Major and Sinai: the hierarchical model and its exponents

This is the reading map for the group of works that made Dyson's hierarchical model the standard
laboratory of the renormalisation group. The division of labour is clear enough to state in one
paragraph. Bleher and Sinai turned the block-spin recursion into a nonlinear transformation of
probability measures and computed the critical indices from its linearisation; Bleher then
constructed self-similar fields with hierarchical structure, which are the fixed points of the
transformation in the language of random fields; Bleher and Major extended the analysis to vector
spins, to the ordered phase with its limit theorems, and to the marginal exponent at which the
criterion for a transition becomes a condition on the level sequence; and Sinai's monograph is the
systematic account of the framework of limit Gibbs distributions in which the model sits.

The wiki uses the group in three ways: as the source of the recursion and of the fixed-point
theorem, as the source of the exponent values and their status, and as the source of the phenomena
that distinguish the vector from the scalar case. No technical estimate of these papers is
reconstructed here; statements are attributed to the works that establish them.

## What is taken from each

### The transformation and the indices

The object of study is the law of the block spin, and the recursion says that the law at one scale
determines the law at the next by a Gibbs tilt of a product measure. The wiki takes the identity
and the point of view from the 1973 paper, states the identity as an exact finite-volume
computation with the free energy as a cocycle, and takes the identification of the critical point
as the boundary between two domains of attraction. The indices are read off the linearisation at
the fixed point, which is the content of the 1975 paper; the wiki separates the dictionary, which
is algebra, from the model-specific inputs to it.

### The fields

The construction of self-similar fields is the probabilistic form of the fixed-point theorem: a
law of the block spin that reproduces itself under decimation, assembled over the scales, is a
field whose block sums have the same law after rescaling. The wiki states the two as two halves of
one orbit rather than as separate results, and cites the construction for the field form.

### The vector papers

The 1984 paper is the ordered phase: a central limit theorem with two normalisations, the square
root of the volume along the magnetisation and a faster rate in the orthogonal directions, which
is the enhancement produced by the transverse modes. The 1985 paper supplies the large-deviation
tool. The 1988 paper treats the non-Gaussian case, in which the large-scale limit is not Gaussian.
The 1990 note is the conference account. The 1987 survey is the place where the model is presented
as a problem about limit distributions of strongly dependent random variables, with the vector
phenomena, the translation invariant states and the conjectures; it records that for vector models
the limits below criticality are not central. The 2021 paper is the quantitative statement at the
marginal exponent, and the wiki states its three claims and does not go beyond them.

### The monograph and the history

The monograph supplies the framework - limit Gibbs distributions, the specification, the place of
the hierarchical model in the general theory - and is cited where the framework is invoked and
nowhere for a quantitative statement. The historical paper is used once, for the history of the
Moscow seminar.

## Outside this map

Three things are deliberately not covered. The proofs of the survey are sketches, and the wiki
does not present them as proofs. The monograph's general theory is used as a framework, not
reconstructed. And no locator is claimed for any work: the wiki cites by work, and the statements
attributed to these papers are those of their published abstracts, reviews or titles, as recorded
in zbMATH Open. Reconciling locators against the printed editions is a separate task, and for the
two 1973 and 1975 papers it needs the printed volumes: no zbMATH record for them was found under
either spelling of the authors' names, so they are cited by DOI only.

Background rather than part of the group: Dyson's 1969 paper, which introduced the couplings and
proved the transition, and the classical long-range exponent values of Fisher, Ma and Nickel,
which the wiki uses as the values of the Gaussian fixed point.
