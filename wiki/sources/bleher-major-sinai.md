# Bleher, Major and Sinai: the hierarchical model and its exponents

This is the reading map for the group of works that made Dyson's hierarchical model the standard
laboratory of the renormalisation group. Its division of labour is clear enough to state in one
paragraph. Bleher and Sinai turned the block-spin recursion into a nonlinear transformation of
probability measures and computed the critical indices from its linearisation; Bleher and Major
extended the analysis to vector spins, to the low-temperature phase and to the marginal exponent,
where the criterion for a transition becomes a condition on the level sequence; and Sinai's
monograph is the systematic account of the framework of limit Gibbs distributions in which the
hierarchical model is the worked example.

The wiki uses this literature in three ways: as the source of the objects of the recursion and of
the fixed-point theorem, as the source of the exponent values and their status, and as the source
of the phenomena that distinguish the vector from the scalar case. Nothing in the wiki
reconstructs the technical estimates of these papers; the statements are attributed to the works
that establish them.

## What is taken from each

### The 1973 paper: the transformation

The object of study is the law of the block spin, and the recursion is the statement that the law
at one scale determines the law at the next by a Gibbs tilt of a product measure. The wiki takes
the identity and the point of view from this paper, and states the identity in the form of an
exact finite-volume computation, with the free energy as a cocycle.

The paper's identification of the critical point as the value of the coupling at which the orbit
changes its domain of attraction is the origin of the trichotomy stated in the fixed-point
article; the wiki states the trichotomy as a theorem of the 1973 and 1975 papers together rather
than as two separate results, because the finite-dimensional statement and its proof are split
between them.

### The 1975 paper: the indices

The indices are read off the spectrum of the linearisation at the fixed point, and the paper
organises the computation around the two relevant directions. The wiki takes the eigenvalue-to-
exponent dictionary from this source and separates what the dictionary needs from what the model
supplies: the growth exponent is fixed by the autonomy of the recursion in this model, and the
correlation-length exponent is the leading eigenvalue.

### The monograph

The monograph supplies the framework rather than any particular estimate: the notion of a limit
Gibbs distribution, the role of the specification, and the place of the hierarchical model within
the general theory of phase transitions. The wiki cites it where the framework is invoked and
nowhere for a quantitative statement.

### The vector papers

The 1984 paper is the ordered phase: a nonzero magnetisation, and transverse modes that stay
massless. The wiki uses it for the mechanism by which the angular direction becomes marginal, and
not for any estimate. The 1987 survey is the place where the model is presented as a problem
about limit distributions of strongly dependent random variables, with the vector phenomena and
the conjectures listed; the wiki uses it for that framing and for the statement that the vector
case shows phenomena absent from the scalar one. The 2021 paper is the quantitative statement
about the marginal exponent: a transition exactly when the level couplings are not summable, a
continuous transition, and a uniform limit law for the direction. The wiki states these three
claims as one theorem and does not go beyond them.

### The historical paper

Used once, for the history of the seminar and of the method.

## Outside this map

Three things are deliberately not covered. The proofs of the 1987 survey are sketches, and the
wiki does not present them as proofs. The monograph's general theory of phase transitions is used
only as a framework, not reconstructed. And no page-level or theorem-level locator is claimed for
any of these works: the wiki cites them by work, and the statements attributed to them are those
of their published abstracts and titles. Reconciling locators against the printed editions is a
separate task.

Background rather than part of the group: Dyson's 1969 paper, which introduced the couplings and
proved the transition, and the classical long-range exponent values of Fisher, Ma and Nickel,
which the wiki uses as the values of the Gaussian fixed point.
